# Resources — mod-006 Pricing, Packaging, and Monetization as a Product Surface

Primary sources for the material in this module. Prefer the books over
the blog posts for depth; prefer the essays and online calculators when
you need a single-sitting refresher or a working tool. Every URL below
is either the author's canonical page, the publisher's page, the
official specification host, or an archived copy where the original has
moved or lapsed.

## Pricing as product design (Ramanujam, Simon-Kucher)

- Madhavan Ramanujam & Georg Tacke, *Monetizing Innovation: How Smart
  Companies Design the Product Around the Price*, Wiley, 2016 —
  [wiley.com/en-us/Monetizing+Innovation](https://www.wiley.com/en-us/Monetizing+Innovation%3A+How+Smart+Companies+Design+the+Product+Around+the+Price-p-9781119240860)
  and
  [simon-kucher.com](https://www.simon-kucher.com/en/insights/monetizing-innovation-book).
  The canonical text for pricing-as-product-design. Chapter 1 is the
  source of the four monetization-failure shapes — feature shocks,
  minivations, hidden gems, undead products — used in
  [Lecture 1](lectures/01-pricing-as-a-product-surface.md) and
  [Exercise 1](exercises/exercise-01-monetization-model-authoring-for-one-product.md).
  Chapters 3–5 are the willingness-to-pay discipline cited in
  [Lecture 2](lectures/02-willingness-to-pay-research.md); Chapter 6
  is the good / better / best discipline cited in
  [Lecture 3](lectures/03-packaging-structure-and-metering.md) and
  [Exercise 2](exercises/exercise-02-good-better-best-packaging-drill.md).
- Simon-Kucher & Partners, consulting-practice writing on pricing
  strategy — [simon-kucher.com/en/insights](https://www.simon-kucher.com/en/insights).
  The firm Ramanujam / Tacke write from; the broader library is the
  practitioner-depth version of the book.
- Simon-Kucher & Partners, *Global Pricing Study*, published annually —
  [simon-kucher.com](https://www.simon-kucher.com/en). The industry
  benchmark on how often companies raise prices, how many run
  structured price research, and how many hit their revenue-lift
  target after a price change. Cited in Lecture 1's framing of why
  pricing-as-product-surface is a founding-CPO job.

## Van Westendorp and Gabor-Granger (the primary WTP instruments)

- Peter H. van Westendorp, "NSS-Price Sensitivity Meter (PSM) — A New
  Approach to Study Consumer Perception of Prices," 1976 ESOMAR
  Congress Proceedings —
  [ana.esomar.org](https://ana.esomar.org/documents/nss-price-sensitivity-meter-psm-a-new-approach-to-study-consumer-perception-of-prices).
  The original paper introducing the four-question price-sensitivity
  meter — too-expensive, too-cheap, expensive, bargain — used through
  [Lecture 2](lectures/02-willingness-to-pay-research.md) and
  [Exercise 3](exercises/exercise-03-van-westendorp-and-gabor-granger-drill.md).
  The four intersection points (PMC, PME, OPP, IPP) and the Range of
  Acceptable Prices come straight from this paper.
- André Gabor & C. W. J. Granger, "Price as an Indicator of Quality:
  Report on an Enquiry," *Economica*, 33(129), 1966 —
  [jstor.org/stable/2552031](https://www.jstor.org/stable/2552031).
  The source paper for the purchase-intent laddering method that
  carries their names, cited in Lecture 2 and Exercise 3.
- Sawtooth Software, "Conjoint Analysis" learning hub —
  [sawtoothsoftware.com/conjoint-analysis](https://sawtoothsoftware.com/conjoint-analysis).
  The practitioner reference for conjoint and discrete-choice analysis
  Lecture 2 names as the depth-above-Gabor-Granger instrument;
  Sawtooth is the market-standard tooling and their documentation is
  the easiest starting point for the method's shape and the N-of-
  respondents needs.

## Pricing experimentation discipline (Kohavi, Miller, Johari, Stucchio)

- Ron Kohavi, Diane Tang & Ya Xu, *Trustworthy Online Controlled
  Experiments: A Practical Guide to A/B Testing*, Cambridge
  University Press, 2020 —
  [experimentguide.com](https://experimentguide.com/). Chapter 2
  (hypothesis structure), Chapter 3 (decision rules), Chapter 17
  (statistical methods), Chapter 21 (guardrails and SRM), Chapter 22
  (Simpson's paradox / segment breakouts) are the base A/B
  discipline
  [Lecture 5](lectures/05-pricing-experiments-and-grandfathering.md)
  and
  [Exercise 4](exercises/exercise-04-pricing-experiment-design-with-guard-metrics.md)
  specialize for pricing experiments. The full base discipline is
  taught in
  [mod-005 Lecture 4](../mod-005-metrics-experimentation-and-ai-evals/lectures/04-experimentation-at-pre-seed-to-series-a.md);
  this module adds the pricing-specific additions.
- Evan Miller, "Sample Size Calculator" —
  [evanmiller.org/ab-testing/sample-size.html](https://www.evanmiller.org/ab-testing/sample-size.html).
  The canonical online calculator for MDE sizing on proportion
  metrics; linked throughout
  [Exercise 4](exercises/exercise-04-pricing-experiment-design-with-guard-metrics.md).
- Evan Miller, "How Not to Run an A/B Test," 2010 —
  [evanmiller.org/how-not-to-run-an-ab-test.html](https://www.evanmiller.org/how-not-to-run-an-ab-test.html).
  The canonical short read on peeking / optional-stopping failure,
  referenced through Lecture 5's "peeking on pricing experiments"
  failure mode.
- Ramesh Johari, Pete Koomen, Leonid Pekelis & David Walsh, "Peeking
  at A/B Tests: Why it matters, and what to do about it," KDD 2017
  (expanded version on arXiv, 2017) —
  [arxiv.org/abs/1512.04922](https://arxiv.org/abs/1512.04922). The
  theoretical foundation for the Optimizely / Statsig / Eppo
  sequential-testing "Stats Engine" style that Lecture 5's and
  Exercise 4's low-traffic default rests on.
- Chris Stucchio, "Easy Evaluation of Decision Rules in Bayesian A/B
  Testing," VWO whitepaper, 2015 —
  [chrisstucchio.com/pubs/VWO_SmartStats_technical_whitepaper.pdf](https://www.chrisstucchio.com/pubs/VWO_SmartStats_technical_whitepaper.pdf).
  The practitioner-level reference for Bayesian A/B with weakly-
  informative priors as the small-N pricing-experiment alternative to
  frequentist fixed-horizon.
- `statsmodels` power / sample-size documentation —
  [statsmodels.org/stable/stats.html#power-and-sample-size-calculations](https://www.statsmodels.org/stable/stats.html#power-and-sample-size-calculations).
  The Python reference for continuous-metric (ARPU, revenue-per-user)
  MDE sizing that Exercise 4 points at for the continuous side of the
  calculation.
