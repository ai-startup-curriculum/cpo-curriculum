# Resources — mod-002 Opportunity Assessment and Ruthless Prioritization

Primary sources for the material in this module. Prefer the books over
the blog posts for depth; prefer the essays and HBR articles when you
need a single-sitting refresher. Every URL below is either the author's
canonical page, the publisher's page, or an archived copy where the
original has moved or lapsed.

## Opportunity assessment and authoring (Cagan, Torres, Blank)

- Marty Cagan, *Inspired: How to Create Tech Products Customers Love*
  (2nd ed.), Wiley, 2017 —
  [SVPG book page](https://www.svpg.com/inspired-how-to-create-products-customers-love/).
  Chapter 22, "Opportunity Assessment Technique," is the source for the
  four-sentence opportunity-assessment shape used throughout
  [Lecture 1](lectures/01-opportunities-as-bounded-problems.md) and
  [Exercise 1](exercises/exercise-01-opportunity-assessment-authoring-for-three-real-bets.md).
- Marty Cagan, "Product Manager Prep Work: Opportunity Assessment,"
  Silicon Valley Product Group, 2007 —
  [svpg.com/product-manager-prep-work-opportunity-assessment](https://www.svpg.com/product-manager-prep-work-opportunity-assessment/).
  The original blog-post statement of the opportunity-assessment
  questions; the shortest single introduction.
- Teresa Torres, *Continuous Discovery Habits*, Product Talk, 2021 —
  [producttalk.org/continuous-discovery-habits](https://www.producttalk.org/continuous-discovery-habits/).
  Chapter 6, "Mapping the Opportunity Space," is the reference for the
  opportunity solution tree and the *customer-worded opportunity node*
  discipline in Lecture 1 and Exercise 1.
- Teresa Torres, "Why I Use Opportunity Solution Trees," Product Talk,
  2016 —
  [producttalk.org/2016/08/opportunity-solution-tree](https://www.producttalk.org/2016/08/opportunity-solution-tree/).
  The original blog post; ten-minute orientation.
- Steve Blank & Bob Dorf, *The Startup Owner's Manual*, K&S Ranch, 2012 —
  [Strategyzer library entry](https://www.strategyzer.com/library/the-startup-owners-manual).
  The problem-interview / solution-interview distinction that grounds
  the "in their words" evidence standard in Lecture 1. Also cross-
  referenced from mod-001.

## Scoring schemes (RICE, ICE, WSJF, Kano)

- Sean McBride, "RICE: Simple prioritization for product managers,"
  Intercom, 2016 —
  [intercom.com/blog/rice-simple-prioritization-for-product-managers](https://www.intercom.com/blog/rice-simple-prioritization-for-product-managers/).
  The canonical statement of RICE, including Intercom's suggested
  ordinal anchors and the divide-by-effort structure that
  [Lecture 2](lectures/02-scoring-schemes-and-their-incompatibilities.md)
  interrogates.
- Sean Ellis & Morgan Brown, *Hacking Growth*, Currency, 2017 —
  [hachettebookgroup.com](https://www.hachettebookgroup.com/titles/sean-ellis/hacking-growth/9780451497215/).
  Chapter 6, "Testing at High Tempo," on ICE as a growth-experiment
  triage tool used with a scoring session, not as a solo number
  generator.
- GrowthHackers community, "How to Prioritize Your Growth Ideas: ICE
  Score" —
  [growthhackers.com](https://growthhackers.com/articles/growth-hacking-ice-score-to-prioritize-growth-ideas).
  Practitioner-audience statement of ICE.
- Don Reinertsen, *The Principles of Product Development Flow*,
  Celeritas Publishing, 2009 —
  [celeritaspublishing.com](https://celeritaspublishing.com/product/the-principles-of-product-development-flow/).
  Chapter 5, "Managing Queues," is the source for the Cost of Delay
  framing and the WSJF rate cited in Lecture 2 and Exercise 2.
- Scaled Agile Framework, "Weighted Shortest Job First" —
  [framework.scaledagile.com/wsjf](https://framework.scaledagile.com/wsjf/).
  SAFe's operational form of Reinertsen's WSJF, including the
  Fibonacci-anchored relative-CoD scoring used in practice.
- Noriaki Kano, Nobuhiku Seraku, Fumio Takahashi & Shinichi Tsuji,
  "Attractive Quality and Must-Be Quality," *Journal of the Japanese
  Society for Quality Control*, Vol. 14, No. 2, 1984 — the English
  translation is reprinted in *The Best on Quality*, IAQ, 1996. See
  the [ASQ summary at
  asq.org/quality-resources/kano-model](https://asq.org/quality-resources/kano-model).
  The primary source for the Kano classification used in Lecture 2 and
  Exercise 2.
- S. S. Stevens, "On the Theory of Scales of Measurement," *Science*,
  Vol. 103, No. 2684, June 1946 —
  [jstor.org/stable/1671815](https://web.archive.org/web/20240128034023/https://www.jstor.org/stable/1671815).
  Stevens's original taxonomy of nominal, ordinal, interval, and
  ratio scales; the reference for the "you can't multiply ordinals"
  point in Lecture 2.

## Base-rate vs strategic decisions (outside view, horizons)

- Daniel Kahneman, *Thinking, Fast and Slow*, Farrar, Straus and
  Giroux, 2011 —
  [us.macmillan.com](https://us.macmillan.com/books/9780374533557/thinkingfastandslow).
  Chapter 23, "The Outside View," is the source for the base-rate /
  reference-class framing in [Lecture 3](lectures/03-base-rate-vs-strategic-opportunities.md).
- Bent Flyvbjerg, "From Nobel Prize to Project Management: Getting
  Risks Right," *Project Management Journal*, August 2006 —
  [arXiv:1302.3642 preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/1302.3642).
  Reference-class forecasting as the operational form of Kahneman's
  outside view, applied to large project decisions.
- Andrew Grove, *High Output Management*, Vintage, 1983 / 1995 —
  [penguinrandomhouse.com](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/158477/high-output-management-by-andrew-s-grove/).
  Chapter 4 (on high-leverage activities and the exception principle)
  is the reference for the "strategic decisions the CPO must make
  personally" distinction cited in Lecture 3.
- Mehrdad Baghai, Steve Coley & David White, *The Alchemy of Growth*,
  Basic Books, 2000; see the McKinsey enduring-ideas summary at
  [mckinsey.com](https://www.mckinsey.com/business-functions/strategy-and-corporate-finance/our-insights/enduring-ideas-the-three-horizons-of-growth).
  The Three Horizons framing cited in Lecture 3 for the different
  cadences horizons deserve.

## Bias mechanics and kill discipline

- Richard Thaler, "Toward a Positive Theory of Consumer Choice,"
  *Journal of Economic Behavior & Organization*, Vol. 1, Issue 1,
  March 1980 —
  [ScienceDirect entry](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/0167268180900517).
  The formalization of the sunk-cost fallacy cited in
  [Lecture 5](lectures/05-killing-work-you-already-started.md).
- Hal R. Arkes & Catherine Blumer, "The Psychology of Sunk Cost,"
  *Organizational Behavior and Human Decision Processes*, Vol. 35,
  No. 1, February 1985 —
  [PDF via MSU](https://msu.edu/~ema/803/Ch11-Uncertainty/2/ArkesBlumer85.pdf).
  Experimental grounding of the sunk-cost effect in decision-making.
- Daniel Kahneman, Jack L. Knetsch & Richard H. Thaler, "Experimental
  Tests of the Endowment Effect and the Coase Theorem," *Journal of
  Political Economy*, Vol. 98, No. 6, December 1990 —
  [JSTOR](https://www.jstor.org/stable/2937761).
  Primary source for the endowment effect used in Lecture 5.
- Barry M. Staw, "Knee-Deep in the Big Muddy: A Study of Escalating
  Commitment to a Chosen Course of Action," *Organizational Behavior
  and Human Performance*, Vol. 16, No. 1, June 1976 —
  [ScienceDirect entry](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/003050737690005U).
  The classic escalation-of-commitment paper; the empirical spine of
  the consistency-bias discussion.
- Robert B. Cialdini, *Influence: The Psychology of Persuasion*
  (revised edition), Harper Business, 2006 —
  [influenceatwork.com](https://www.influenceatwork.com/). Chapter 3,
  "Commitment and Consistency," is the reference for the consistency
  bias in Lecture 5.
- Daniel Kahneman & Amos Tversky, "Prospect Theory: An Analysis of
  Decision under Risk," *Econometrica*, Vol. 47, No. 2, March 1979 —
  [JSTOR](https://www.jstor.org/stable/1914185).
  The primary source for loss aversion; the empirical grounding for
  the "swap-framing" counter-move in Lecture 5.
- Charlie Munger, "The Psychology of Human Misjudgment," Harvard Law
  School speech, 1995 — transcribed at
  [fs.blog/great-talks/psychology-human-misjudgment](https://fs.blog/great-talks/psychology-human-misjudgment/).
  Munger's marginal-decision test — *"if I hadn't already started
  this, would I start it now?"* — cited in Lecture 5.
- Jim Collins, *Good to Great*, HarperBusiness, 2001 —
  [jimcollins.com](https://www.jimcollins.com/books/good-to-great.html).
  Chapter 6, "A Culture of Discipline," is the source for the
  stop-doing list as a governance ritual, adapted to the kill list in
  Lecture 5 and Exercise 4.
- Jim Collins, "Best New Year's Resolution? A 'Stop Doing' List,"
  2003 —
  [jimcollins.com/article_topics/articles/best-new-years.html](https://www.jimcollins.com/article_topics/articles/best-new-years.html).
  Short-form companion to the *Good to Great* chapter.
- Gary Klein, "Performing a Project Premortem," *Harvard Business
  Review*, September 2007 —
  [hbr.org/2007/09/performing-a-project-premortem](https://hbr.org/2007/09/performing-a-project-premortem).
  The pre-mortem technique cited in Lecture 5 as the mechanism for
  authoring kill criteria at fund time.

that show up in most founding-CPO reading lists on prioritization:

  [oreilly.com](https://www.oreilly.com/library/view/escaping-the-build/9781491973783/).
  Outcome-orientation and product-org anti-patterns; useful bridge
  between mod-002 (prioritization) and mod-003 (roadmap).

  the Face of Uncertainty*, self-published, 2024 —
  [itamargilad.com/book](https://itamargilad.com/book/). The ICE →
  ConfidenceMeter progression; a useful companion when you want to
  push past ICE without adopting SAFe.
