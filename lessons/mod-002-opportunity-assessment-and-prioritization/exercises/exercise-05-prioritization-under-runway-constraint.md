# Exercise 5 — Prioritization under a runway constraint

**Time:** ~2 hours. **Deliverable:** three prioritization memos —
one per runway scenario — plus a one-page prioritization-context
sheet.

## Purpose

Practice adjusting the ranking, the bet count, and the kill discipline
as the runway constraint tightens. The lecture behind this is
[Lecture 6](../lectures/06-thesis-and-runway-as-boundaries.md).

## The scenario

Take the product and the opportunity list you built in Exercise 1 (the
three opportunity assessments) or Exercise 3 (the eight-opportunity
spillover table). Assume the following thesis is stable across the
three runway scenarios:

> *"We are the workflow product for [segment you named]. Our theory of
> winning is faster-than-competitor discovery loops with the target
> segment, funded by usage-based revenue from that segment's power
> users. We are not chasing enterprise until the segment loves us."*

Adapt the thesis to your product if you need to; keep it stable across
the three scenarios.

## The three runway scenarios

For the same opportunity list, produce three prioritization memos —
one for each of the following runway conditions:

### Runway A — 22 months, $1.2M / month burn, series A recently closed

- 2–3 concurrent strategic bets are fundable.
- Base-rate work has its own capacity allocation.
- Runway is long enough that a bet that finishes in Q3 has time to
  prove out before you'd raise the next round.

### Runway B — 12 months, $900k / month burn, planning a bridge or a Series B

- One concurrent strategic bet at a time.
- Base-rate work has to visibly move fundraising-relevant metrics.
- A bet that misses at its Q2 checkpoint has to have a clean kill
  and a redeploy in time to still show a story at the raise.

### Runway C — 7 months, $700k / month burn, cost cuts already in

- Strategic bets are dangerous; anything that doesn't shorten the
  path to closing the round or reaching break-even is deferred.
- Pricing, packaging, and monetization moves may outrank product
  bets — this is where mod-006 material starts to dominate the
  queue.
- The kill discipline is at its highest: anything not
  fundraising-material or break-even-material is a kill candidate.

## Tasks

For **each of the three runway scenarios**:

1. **Rewrite the top of the queue.** Which opportunities from your
   Exercise-1 / Exercise-3 list are the top 1–3 items under this
   runway? Which fall below the line?
2. **Name the strategic bets you're funding.** Zero, one, two, or
   three, matching the runway's implied bet count.
3. **Name what's *not* getting funded that you'd like to.** Being
   explicit about the tradeoff is the discipline; a memo that
   doesn't name the sacrifice is dishonest.
4. **Give the kill rule.** Every funded strategic bet needs a
   pre-registered kill criterion sized to the runway — the shorter
   the runway, the earlier and tighter the checkpoint.
5. **Note any thesis stress.** If honestly prioritizing against this
   runway forces you to act against the thesis, name the mismatch
   and — following Lecture 6 — the escalation. Do not silently
   discount the thesis.

Then produce a **one-page prioritization-context sheet** in the
shape from
[Lecture 6](../lectures/06-thesis-and-runway-as-boundaries.md#the-two-boundaries-on-one-page):

- **Thesis extract** (top): the two or three sentences of thesis
  you're prioritizing against.
- **Runway line** (middle): current runway in months, monthly burn,
  authored by (a real name / role), dated. Compute the "concurrent
  strategic bets we can fund" number.
- **Prioritization implication** (bottom): two or three sentences
  translating thesis + runway into the current allocation and bet
  count.

Do this three times — one for each runway. The point is to feel
how the same thesis and the same opportunity list produce three
very different rankings.

## Starter guidance

- **Runway A is the training-wheels scenario.** Most CPO writing
  online implicitly assumes this state. Notice how much flexibility
  you have; then compare against B and C to feel where the
  flexibility comes from.
- **Runway B is where the CPO earns their keep.** The queue is
  actually constrained, but the company is not yet in survival mode
  — the strategic bet choice matters most here.
- **Runway C is a role change.** The CPO's queue in Runway C reads
  more like a monetization + fundraising-support queue than a
  discovery queue. That is not a demotion; it is the shape of the
  job at that moment. Fighting it is how founding CPOs get
  themselves fired in the wrong quarter.
- **Never silently downgrade the thesis.** Lecture 6's escalation
  rule is: if the runway does not fund the thesis, name the
  mismatch to the founder-CEO in writing. Practice writing that
  paragraph in Runway C, even if it feels harsh.

## Acceptance criteria

- Three prioritization memos, each ≤ one page.
- Each memo names the top 1–3 items, the specific items *not*
  funded that you'd like to, and pre-registered kill rules per
  strategic bet.
- Each memo notes any thesis-runway mismatch and, where one exists,
  drafts the escalation sentence to the founder-CEO.
- One prioritization-context sheet per runway, following the
  Lecture 6 shape (thesis / runway / implication).
- The three memos differ meaningfully in what they fund. If Runway
  A and Runway C produce the same top three, you have not
  compressed hard enough — retry.

## Common failure modes

- **Same ranking, tighter timelines.** A common mis-solution is to
  keep the same bets and just shrink the timeline. The point is
  that some bets *should not be started* under Runway B or C, not
  that all bets should be crammed.
- **Refusing to name what you're not funding.** A memo that lists
  what you're doing without listing what you're not doing has
  hidden the tradeoff; the tradeoff is the exercise.
- **Silent thesis discount.** Prioritizing against Runway C by
  quietly ignoring the "not chasing enterprise" clause of the
  thesis. Lecture 6 — do not do this; name the mismatch.
- **Fundraising-adjacent items dressed up as customer bets.** In
  Runway C, "let's ship the announceable thing" is a real move,
  and it should be named as *fundraising-material* honestly, not
  laundered as customer-facing prioritization.
