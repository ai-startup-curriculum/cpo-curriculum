# Exercise 7 — Product-progress board-slice authoring

**Time:** ~2 hours. **Deliverable:** one three-
part product-progress board slice, one divergence-
with-fundraise-narrative spec, one board-1:1
plan, and a reviewer's memo — for the current or
most recent quarter.

## Purpose

Author the CPO's slice of the board narrative
from
[Lecture 7](../lectures/07-product-progress-for-the-board.md)
for one quarter. The deliverable is the exact
artifact the CPO hands to the CEO at least one
week before a board meeting — the three-section
shape (what we learned, what we shipped, what
we're doing next), one graph per section, real
numbers cited.

The target failure mode this exercise drills
against is the Lecture 7 §"Common failure modes"
status-report shape — the slice that reads as a
list of things the team did, with no learning
connective tissue and no honest naming of what
didn't work. The counter-move is the explicit
discipline of leading with learning, matching
ships to bets, and naming contradictions and
stops.

## Choosing the quarter

Pick one quarter you can author against with
real material. In order of preference:

1. **The current quarter at the company you
   lead product at.** Live stakes; the slice is
   the real artifact.
2. **A recent quarter from your last role**, or
   a quarter you watched a founding team run.
3. **A plausible synthetic.** If you need to
   fabricate, pick a real-looking AI-native
   company shape (Series A, 15–40 people, one
   AI-native product line, 2–3 strategic bets
   on the quarter's roadmap) and write a one-
   paragraph setup: product, stage, board
   composition (2–4 members, including the
   lead investor and whether any is an ex-
   product operator), fundraise posture
   (planning a round / recently raised / not
   fundraising), and the quarter's two or
   three bets from the strategy (mod-003).

Whichever you pick, name the board specifically.
Writing for "the board" without naming the three
to five members produces a generic slice; writing
for "Priya from Benchmark (lead), Marcus (ex-
Stripe PM), and the founder" produces a specific
one.

## What the artifact bundle must contain

Roughly 4–5 pages total — the slice itself is
2–4 pages, with a smaller divergence spec, board-
1:1 plan, and reviewer's memo on top.

### 1. The product-progress slice (2–4 pages or 6–8 slides)

The exact three-section shape from
[Lecture 7 §"The three-part product-progress narrative"](../lectures/07-product-progress-for-the-board.md#the-three-part-product-progress-narrative).
Pick memo-form (2–3 pages) for pre-seed / seed
boards or slide-form (6–8 slides) for Series A
onward; note which form you picked and why.

**Section 1 — What we learned (one page / 2–3
slides).**

- Two to four specific learnings, each in one
  paragraph.
- The evidence per learning (interview count
  and segment per mod-001; analytics surface
  and cohort per mod-005; experiment or eval
  identifier). Cite specifically.
- The bet the learning changes — what we
  believed before, what we now believe, what
  we're doing differently.
- At least one learning that *contradicted*
  the team's prior belief. Per Lecture 7
  §"Section 1" — contradicting learnings
  matter most. If every learning confirmed
  the prior belief, the slice reads as spin.
- One graph (the retention curve, the
  learning-driven cohort split, the
  interview-coded theme chart) that supports
  the load-bearing learning.

**Section 2 — What we shipped (one page / 2–3
slides).**

- Two to four significant ships, grouped by
  the bet they served.
- The bet connection explicit, anchored to the
  mod-003 strategy. Ships that happened
  outside the strategy are named with the
  reason (security incident, customer
  commitment, kill decision).
- The outcome per ship — adoption, activation,
  retention, revenue — from the mod-005
  analytics regime. Early-and-pending
  outcomes are named as such.
- At least one ship that *didn't produce its
  expected outcome*. Per Lecture 7 §"Section
  2" — 100%-success slides read as spin.
- One graph (the launch dashboard summary,
  the adoption curve for the biggest ship,
  the activation delta from the biggest
  change) that supports the load-bearing
  ship.

**Section 3 — What we're doing next (one page
/ 2–3 slides).**

- Two to four biggest bets for next quarter,
  each in one paragraph, anchored to the
  mod-003 roadmap.
- Named risks per bet. At least one risk per
  bet that is *not* known-solvable; the
  honest kind that the board can discount
  if it's missing.
- One to two things the team is *stopping*
  or *deferring* — mod-002 kill-list
  applied to the board narrative. Per
  Lecture 7, this is the most under-used
  signal of judgment.
- One or two specific asks of the board —
  intro, feedback, network. Boards want to
  help; they need to be told how.
- One graph (the quarter's roadmap on a
  timeline, the budget split between bets,
  the eval-trajectory projection for an AI
  feature) that supports the next-quarter
  plan.

**Formatting commitments** (from Lecture 7
§"The artifact form"):

- Real numbers cited, not "engagement is up."
- One graph per section maximum.
- No jargon the board doesn't speak. "MRR
  compound from top-of-funnel PLG
  optimization" is a sentence the board will
  pretend to understand; say it in normal
  English.
- Delivered to the CEO at least one week
  before the board meeting. If you draft
  this in Google Doc or Notion, timestamp
  the handoff.

### 2. The divergence-with-fundraise-narrative spec (half page)

Per
[Lecture 7 §"The relationship to the CEO's fundraise narrative"](../lectures/07-product-progress-for-the-board.md#the-relationship-to-the-ceos-fundraise-narrative).
Even if there's no active fundraise, name the
likely divergences the CEO will want to apply
to the slice:

- **The specific claim(s) you expect the CEO
  to want framed differently.** An activation
  number that would benefit from omitting a
  caveat, a "we shipped X" claim where X
  isn't in GA, a competitive comparison that
  flatters your product. Name 1–3 candidate
  claims.
- **The caveat the CPO version includes that
  the fundraise version might omit.** Written
  out.
- **The Part 1 move** — the specific sentence
  you'd use in the CPO / CEO 1:1 the week
  before the board meeting to surface the
  divergence privately. Not confrontational;
  co-authoring the narrative.
- **The Part 2 fallback.** If the CEO insists
  on the stronger framing, what's the honest
  answer you'd give if a board member turns
  to you and asks directly. Per Lecture 7
  §"Part 2 — Name what you can't say" — not
  throwing the CEO under the bus, but not
  endorsing a claim you can't defend.
- **The escalation trigger.** If the pattern
  recurs across quarters, the Lecture 1
  escalation path applies. Name the trigger
  that would take the pattern from "normal
  co-authoring" to "escalation."

### 3. The board-1:1 plan (half page)

The Lecture 7 §"The board 1:1" discipline. Even
if you're pre-Series-A and the practice doesn't
yet apply, name the board-member relationship
you'd build *first* once it does.

- **Target board member.** Who and why — the
  lead investor, the ex-CPO on the board,
  someone else specific. Pick one.
- **Cadence.** Monthly or quarterly, 30–45
  minutes.
- **The three ground rules** per Lecture 7:
  (a) tell the CEO the 1:1 is happening, (b)
  don't use it to escalate around the CEO,
  (c) don't ask the board member to make
  decisions the CEO owns. Written in your
  voice.
- **The first-meeting agenda.** The specific
  three or four items you'd bring. Shared-
  context on the product function, open
  decisions where you'd value seasoned
  counsel, the shape of your 90-day plan
  (or where you are inside it).

## Starter guidance

- **Lead with learning, not shipping.**
  Lecture 7 is explicit about the order:
  putting "shipping" first produces the
  status-report failure mode; putting "next"
  first produces the fundraise-argument
  failure mode. Learning first, every time.
- **The contradicting learning is where
  trust compounds.** If you cannot name a
  learning that contradicted a prior belief,
  the quarter either lacked real discovery
  effort (a problem) or the slice is spin (a
  bigger problem). Lecture 7's point about
  the board reading over quarters applies
  specifically here.
- **One graph per section, maximum.** The
  temptation is to over-chart. Boards read
  decks fast; one good graph beats five
  mediocre ones.
- **Name the stops.** The forward-looking
  section is where founding CPOs most
  under-use the honest-admission tool. What
  are you deliberately *not* doing next
  quarter? What did you kill?
- **Normal English.** The jargon test is
  simple: if a board member who is not a
  product operator wouldn't understand the
  sentence, rewrite it. "The MRR compound
  from the top-of-funnel PLG optimization"
  is a sentence Lecture 7 names
  specifically.
- **Deliver one week early.** The night-
  before-handoff anti-pattern from Lecture
  7's "late delivery" failure mode is the
  one most CPOs stumble into. The slice
  land-rate goes way up when the CEO gets
  it with time to fold in.
- **The divergence surfaces privately,
  always.** Lecture 7's two-part discipline:
  Part 1 (surface in the 1:1, co-author) is
  what keeps you out of Part 2 (don't
  endorse under direct question). The
  former works; the latter is a last
  resort.

## The reviewer's memo

Half a page, written after the artifact bundle.
Answer:

- **The three questions test.** Does your
  draft answer: (a) is the product function
  learning? (b) is it building for the
  thesis? (c) is the CPO trustworthy under
  pressure? Point at the specific sections
  that answer each.
- **The honest-admission audit.** Count the
  contradicting learnings (Section 1), the
  underperforming ships (Section 2), and the
  stops (Section 3). If any of the three
  counts is zero, name why — and name whether
  the quarter actually produced none or the
  slice is omitting them.
- **The six-failure-mode self-check.** Which
  of the Lecture 7 §"Common failure modes" is
  your draft most at risk of — status-report
  shape, fundraise-argument shape, no named
  risks, no named stops, only-confirming
  learnings, late delivery? Point at the
  evidence.
- **The CEO reaction.** What specifically
  will the CEO want to change when they read
  this before board? Where do you expect
  pushback — the specific claim, the specific
  omission, the specific caveat?
- **The 4-quarter arc.** This is one slice.
  Lecture 7 is explicit that the trust-build
  is across many slides. What theme or thread
  do you want the board to see developing
  across the next four slices?

## Acceptance criteria

- Slice has all three sections, in order,
  with the Lecture 7 shape and content for
  each.
- Section 1 includes at least one
  contradicting learning with cited evidence.
- Section 2 includes at least one ship that
  didn't produce its expected outcome.
- Section 3 includes at least one named stop
  / deferral and at least one named risk per
  bet that is not known-solvable.
- Each section has one graph, no more.
- Real numbers cited; no jargon the board
  doesn't speak.
- Divergence spec names 1–3 candidate claims,
  the Part 1 sentence, the Part 2 fallback,
  and the escalation trigger.
- Board-1:1 plan names a specific target
  board member, cadence, the three ground
  rules, and a first-meeting agenda.
- Every reference to
  [Lecture 7](../lectures/07-product-progress-for-the-board.md)
  cites the specific section rather than the
  lecture as a whole.
- Reviewer's memo answers all five prompts.

## Common failure modes

- **Status-report shape.** The slice reads
  as a bulleted list of things the team did
  with no learning connective tissue. Fix:
  make Section 1 load-bearing and connect
  Section 2 ships back to Section 1
  learnings.
- **Fundraise-argument shape.** The slice
  overstates and omits caveats. Fix: this
  is the CEO's job, not the CPO's; the
  divergence spec is where you handle it.
- **No contradictions.** Every learning
  confirmed the prior belief; every ship
  worked; nothing was stopped. Fix: either
  the quarter genuinely produced none (a
  problem to name) or the slice is spin
  (a bigger problem).
- **No named stops.** The next-section is
  all new starts. Fix: a founding CPO's
  judgment is most visible in what gets
  killed; the mod-002 kill-list applied to
  the narrative.
- **Jargon.** "The MRR compound from the
  top-of-funnel PLG optimization." Fix: say
  it in English.
- **Over-charting.** Five graphs per
  section. Fix: one graph each; pick the
  best one.
- **Late delivery.** The CPO hands the CEO
  the slice the night before. Fix:
  delivered at least a week early, with a
  Lecture 1 Friday-update-style flag that
  it's ready.
- **No board 1:1 plan.** The exercise skips
  the board-member relationship because
  it's "not yet applicable." Fix: author
  the plan for the member you'd approach
  first *once* it applies. Lecture 7 is
  explicit that this is one of the most
  under-adopted founding-CPO practices.
