# mod-007 — Working with Founders, Engineering, and GTM as the First Product Hire

> The founding CPO's seventh job — the one every other
> module quietly assumed — is **the working relationships
> that make the rest of the job possible**. The direct
> partnership with a technical founder-CEO. The technical
> fluency to argue engineering trade-offs on merit rather
> than by title. The AI/LLM system literacy that lets the
> PRD describe a system the eng team can actually build.
> The influence craft that keeps sales, CS, marketing,
> and design aligned without a reporting line. The scope-
> conflict discipline that resolves the "rewrite vs. ship"
> argument with data instead of authority. The GTM launch
> coordination that stops the "did anyone tell support?"
> mid-launch phone call. The board product-progress
> narrative the CPO writes and the CEO folds into the
> raise. This is the module that turns a talented
> individual PM into a **founding CPO** — someone who
> operates through others rather than around them.

## Why this module exists

Ask a first-time founding CPO what they most under-
prepared for and the answer will not be the discovery
craft, the roadmap craft, the metrics regime, or even
the AI-substrate design surface. It will be the human
system around all of those: *"I didn't know how to
disagree with the CEO productively," "I couldn't get
engineering to take the debt seriously," "sales sold
something that isn't in the roadmap and I found out on
launch day," "I didn't know what the board wanted from
my three slides."* Every one of those is a working-
relationship failure, and every one of them silently
degrades whatever the other six modules taught.

At the pre-seed → Series-B scale a founding CPO
actually operates in, three structural facts make the
relationship craft harder than it is at a mature
company:

- **The CEO is technical and opinionated about product.**
  In 2026 AI-native postings, the CEO is more often
  than not an ex-engineer or ex-PM founder who
  *already has product opinions*. The founding-CPO
  job is a partnership, not an inheritance.
- **There is no functional insulation.** No director
  of engineering to buffer scope conflict, no head of
  sales to translate GTM commitments, no head of
  design to run the design-review calendar. The CPO
  is the seam and the seam has almost no give.
- **The board wants a product-progress narrative
  cadenced to the fundraise, not to the shipping
  calendar.** The CEO owns the raise; the CPO owns
  the product story that fits inside it — and both
  jobs have to be done well for either to work.

This module teaches the founding-CPO version of each
seam. It grounds the framework choices in the standard
practitioner references — Marty Cagan's *Empowered*
model for cross-functional teams and product leadership,
Bain / RAPID and Bridgespan / DACI on decision-rights,
Andrew Grove's *High Output Management* on 1:1 discipline
and delegation, Ryan Singer / Basecamp on scope as an
appetite, Camille Fournier's *The Manager's Path* on
the engineering-management vocabulary the CPO borrows —
and it explicitly links out to the peer engineering,
GTM, and finance curricula for the depth this level
does not own.

## Learning outcomes

By the end of this module you can:

1. **Design and defend a working relationship with a
   technical founder-CEO.** Author a decision-rights
   map (who decides what, unilaterally / with input /
   by consensus), a weekly cadence (1:1, product
   review, written weekly), a tie-breaker (the CEO's
   final call, the CPO's right to be heard), and a
   disagreement / escalation path that keeps
   disagreements *productive* rather than *invisible*.
2. **Build the technical fluency the market asks of a
   founding CPO** — read a PR well enough to ask the
   right question, reason about API and infrastructure
   trade-offs, scope an engineering slice within a
   factor of two, and catch a PRD that under-specifies
   the system before eng discovers it in the sprint.
   Defers to [cto-curriculum](https://github.com/ai-startup-curriculum/cto-curriculum)
   (level 25) for architectural depth.
3. **Reason about AI/LLM system architecture at
   founding-PM depth** — the context pipeline, the
   retrieval design, the tool / MCP contracts, the
   evaluation loop, the cost / latency / quality
   budget. Defers to
   [ai-eval-engineer-learning](https://github.com/ai-engineering-curriculum/ai-eval-engineer-learning),
   [rag-engineer-learning](https://github.com/ai-engineering-curriculum/rag-engineer-learning),
   and [agentic-ai-engineer-learning](https://github.com/ai-engineering-curriculum/agentic-ai-engineer-learning)
   for engineering depth.
4. **Lead cross-functionally through influence rather
   than authority** — Cagan's *empowered product teams*
   as the operating shape, the written pre-alignment
   discipline, the "let me try to convince you" script,
   and the specific anti-patterns (escalation-as-
   influence, parallel-lobbying, silent RACI overrides)
   that dominate at pre-seed scale.
5. **Resolve engineering scope conflict with data
   rather than authority** — the "rewrite vs. ship"
   argument decomposed into the four moves (rewrite,
   refactor-in-place, ship-and-fix-later, kill), the
   technical-debt cost-to-carry evidence consumed
   from [cto-curriculum](https://github.com/ai-startup-curriculum/cto-curriculum)
   mod-105, and the artifact each move demands.
6. **Coordinate a launch with GTM at founding-PM
   scope** — the launch-readiness checklist across
   product, marketing, sales, support, success, and
   telemetry; the go / no-go meeting shape; the
   escalation-to-not-shipping discipline. Defers deep
   enterprise-selling motion to
   [startup-product-gtm-curriculum](https://github.com/ai-startup-curriculum/startup-product-gtm-curriculum)
   (level 30).
7. **Author the product-progress slice of the board
   narrative without owning the fundraise** — a three-
   part slice (what we shipped, what we learned, what
   we're doing next) the CPO writes and the CEO folds
   into the raise deck, plus the disagreement discipline
   for when the fundraise story would distort the
   product-progress claim. Defers to
   [founder-ceo-curriculum](https://github.com/ai-startup-curriculum/founder-ceo-curriculum)
   (level 20) and
   [startup-finance-fundraising-curriculum](https://github.com/ai-startup-curriculum/startup-finance-fundraising-curriculum)
   (level 40) for the fundraise-narrative craft itself.

## How to work this module

Read the lectures in order. Lecture 1 fixes the *most
load-bearing relationship* — the direct partnership
with a technical founder-CEO — because every seam that
follows depends on the decision-rights map, the cadence,
and the escalation path Lecture 1 authors. Lectures 2
and 3 build the *technical and AI-system fluency* the
market asks a founding CPO to bring personally, so
Lecture 5's scope-conflict conversations are grounded
in shared engineering vocabulary rather than an
authority argument. Lecture 4 gives you the *influence
craft* — how to lead sales, CS, marketing, and design
without a reporting line — because Lectures 5 and 6
both assume it. Lecture 5 is the engineering-scope
seam where the CPO / CTO / CEO triangle most often
breaks; it decomposes the "rewrite vs. ship" argument
into a decision framework with named evidence. Lecture
6 gives you the *GTM launch* discipline — the
readiness checklist, the go / no-go, the escalation
to *not* shipping — that separates a coordinated
launch from a support-desk-melts-on-Wednesday launch.
Lecture 7 gives you the *board* seam: the product-
progress narrative you author and the CEO folds into
the fundraise, and the disagreement discipline for
when the two stories diverge.

Do the exercises against real people where you can.
Every exercise in this module benefits enormously
from being run against a real CEO, real engineering
lead, real head of sales, real head of CS. The
synthetic fallbacks work — you can author a decision-
rights map for a plausible team — but the drill
value comes from bringing the artifact to the actual
counterpart and negotiating it. If you're between
roles, use your last team; if you're pre-team, run
each exercise twice: once synthetic, once against a
founder you'd want to work with.

Exercises 1 → 7 build on each other. Exercise 1
authors the CPO / CEO decision-rights map that every
subsequent exercise references. Exercise 2 is the
technical-fluency self-assessment that identifies the
90-day plan for closing your gaps (which then becomes
input to Exercise 5's scope-conflict drill). Exercise
3 is a read-drill on a real AI-substrate system —
your own or an open-source reference — that produces
the reading-order and question-list Chapter 3
teaches. Exercise 4 is a cross-functional-alignment
memo against a real (or plausible) misalignment.
Exercise 5 is a scope-conflict resolution drill on
a rewrite-vs-ship decision. Exercise 6 authors the
launch-readiness plan for a real (or plausible)
launch. Exercise 7 authors the product-progress
board slice for the current or last quarter.
