# Resources — mod-007 Working with Founders, Engineering, and GTM

Primary sources for the material in this module. Prefer the books over
the blog posts for depth; prefer the essays and primary documentation
when you need a single-sitting refresher or a working reference. Every
URL below is either the author's canonical page, the publisher's page,
the official specification host, or the primary documentation site.

## Empowered-product-team shape (Cagan / SVPG)

- Marty Cagan, *Empowered: Ordinary People, Extraordinary Products*,
  Wiley, 2020 —
  [svpg.com/empowered](https://www.svpg.com/empowered-ordinary-people-extraordinary-products/).
  The canonical text for the *empowered product teams* model, product-
  leadership craft, and coaching / staffing discipline. Chapters 8–10
  (product teams, coaching, staffing) are the anchor for
  [Lecture 1](lectures/01-cpo-ceo-working-relationship.md) and
  [Lecture 4](lectures/04-influence-without-authority.md); Chapters
  11–15 extend the shape to the leadership and transformation craft
  this module uses as background.
- Marty Cagan, *Inspired: How to Create Tech Products Customers Love*
  (2nd ed.), Wiley, 2017 —
  [svpg.com/inspired](https://www.svpg.com/inspired-how-to-create-products-customers-love/).
  Part V, "The Right Culture," on product-team culture and cross-
  functional-team shape; referenced in Lectures 1 and 4.
- SVPG (Silicon Valley Product Group) writing, 2010–2026 —
  [svpg.com/articles](https://www.svpg.com/articles/). The ongoing
  practitioner library for empowered-team craft at depth; useful as
  the self-study path alongside *Empowered*.

## 1:1 discipline, delegation, and management output (Grove)

- Andrew S. Grove, *High Output Management* (revised edition),
  Vintage, 1995 —
  [penguinrandomhouse.com](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/158477/high-output-management-by-andrew-s-grove/).
  Chapter 4 (managerial leverage), Chapter 5 (meetings), Chapter 8
  (1:1 discipline), and Chapter 13 (task-relevant maturity /
  delegation) are the vocabulary
  [Lecture 1](lectures/01-cpo-ceo-working-relationship.md) uses for
  the CPO / CEO 1:1 cadence and
  [Lecture 4](lectures/04-influence-without-authority.md) uses for
  delegation depth.

## Decision-rights frameworks (RAPID, DACI)

- Paul Rogers & Marcia Blenko, "Who Has the D? How Clear Decision
  Roles Enhance Organizational Performance," *Harvard Business
  Review*, January 2006 —
  [hbr.org/2006/01/who-has-the-d](https://hbr.org/2006/01/who-has-the-d-how-clear-decision-roles-enhance-organizational-performance).
  The source paper for the RAPID (Recommend, Agree, Perform, Input,
  Decide) decision-rights framing
  [Lecture 1](lectures/01-cpo-ceo-working-relationship.md) adapts to
  the founding-CPO / CEO seam, and that
  [Exercise 1](exercises/exercise-01-cpo-ceo-decision-rights-map.md)
  simplifies to three modes.
- Bain & Company, "RAPID: A tool for setting decision rights" —
  [bain.com/insights/rapid-tool-to-clarify-decision-accountability](https://www.bain.com/insights/rapid-tool-to-clarify-decision-accountability/).
  Bain's practitioner page for the model, with contemporary
  worked examples.
- Bridgespan Group, "The DACI Framework for Decision Making" and
  related writing —
  [bridgespan.org](https://www.bridgespan.org/). The nonprofit-
  sector-authored RACI / DACI framing; useful contrast to RAPID when
  authoring the decision-rights map in Exercise 1.

## Scope-as-appetite and shape-up discipline (Singer / Basecamp)

- Ryan Singer, *Shape Up: Stop Running in Circles and Ship Work That

  [basecamp.com/shapeup](https://basecamp.com/shapeup). "Appetite"

  [Lecture 5](lectures/05-engineering-scope-conflict.md) uses for

  applies in the appetite spec.

## Second-system effect and the rewrite trap (Brooks)

- Fred Brooks, *The Mythical Man-Month: Essays on Software Engineering*
  (anniversary edition), Addison-Wesley, 1995 —
  [informit.com](https://www.informit.com/store/mythical-man-month-the-essays-on-software-engineering-9780201835953).
  "The Second-System Effect" (Chapter 5) and "Plan to Throw One Away"
  (Chapter 11) are the empirical grounding for
  [Lecture 5](lectures/05-engineering-scope-conflict.md)'s
  "engineers want to rewrite" pattern and for the appetite /
  circuit-breaker discipline that prevents it.
