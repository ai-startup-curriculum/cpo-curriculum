# Resources — mod-004 Discovery / Delivery Cadence: Ship Weekly, Sweat Microcopy, Own the Pixel

Primary sources for the material in this module. Prefer the books over
the blog posts for depth; prefer the essays and HBR articles when you
need a single-sitting refresher. Every URL below is either the author's
canonical page, the publisher's page, the official specification host,
or an archived copy where the original has moved or lapsed.

## Dual-track discovery / delivery (Cagan, Torres, Sy, Patton)

- Marty Cagan, *Inspired: How to Create Tech Products Customers Love*
  (2nd ed.), Wiley, 2017 —
  [svpg.com/inspired](https://www.svpg.com/inspired-how-to-create-products-customers-love/).
  Part IV ("The Right Process") is the source for the product-trio
  framing — PM owns value / viability, product designer owns usability,
  tech lead owns feasibility — and the dual-track-as-continuous-rhythm
  argument used throughout
  [Lecture 1](lectures/01-dual-track-discovery-delivery-cadence.md) and
  [Exercise 1](exercises/exercise-01-dual-track-cadence-authoring-for-one-team.md).
  Chapter 8 ("Faux Agile") is the Lecture 6 reference for the
  degenerate-agile anti-pattern.
- Marty Cagan with Chris Jones, *Empowered: Ordinary People,
  Extraordinary Products*, Wiley, 2020 —
  [svpg.com/empowered](https://www.svpg.com/empowered-ordinary-people-extraordinary-products/).
  Chapter 8 ("The Empowered Product Team") is the source for the
  empowered-trio structure Lecture 1 and
  [Lecture 6](lectures/06-ship-velocity-anti-patterns.md) rest on.
  Chapter 9 ("Product Manager") grounds the Lecture 1 split between
  the CPO's discovery work and the delivery queue the CPO does not
  tend. Chapter 10 ("Product Designer") is the direct reference for
  [Lecture 4](lectures/04-own-the-pixel-figma-and-microcopy.md)'s
  product-minded-designer / design-minded-PM pattern. Chapter 24
  ("Product Team Autonomy") supplies
  [Lecture 2](lectures/02-shape-up-vs-weekly-kanban.md)'s stance that
  the choice of operating model is itself an empowered-team decision.
- Teresa Torres, *Continuous Discovery Habits: Discover Products
  that Create Customer Value and Business Value*, Product Talk, 2021 —
  [producttalk.org/continuous-discovery-habits](https://www.producttalk.org/continuous-discovery-habits/).
  Chapter 1 ("The Discovery Habits") is the source for the weekly
  customer-touchpoint cadence Lecture 1 treats as non-negotiable.
  Chapter 3 ("Involve Your Product Trio") grounds the trio-as-unit
  framing. Chapter 6 ("Mapping the Opportunity Space") supplies the
  opportunity solution tree used as the always-live artifact in
  Lecture 1 and Exercise 1.
- Desirée Sy, "Adapting Usability Investigations for Agile User-
  Centered Design," *Journal of Usability Studies*, Vol. 2 Issue 3,
  2007 —
  [uxpajournal.org/adapting-usability-investigations-for-agile-user-centered-design](https://uxpajournal.org/adapting-usability-investigations-for-agile-user-centered-design/).
  Origin of the *dual track* name in the UX-research literature;
  cited in Lecture 1's history-of-the-term section.
- Jeff Patton, "Dual Track Development is Not Duel Track,"
  jpattonassociates.com, 2017 —
  [jpattonassociates.com/dual-track-development](https://www.jpattonassociates.com/dual-track-development/).
  The generalization of Sy's naming into the continuous-discovery /
  continuous-delivery framing used in Lecture 1.

## Delivery operating models — Shape Up and kanban (Singer, Anderson, Reinertsen)

- Ryan Singer, *Shape Up: Stop Running in Circles and Ship Work that
  Matters*, Basecamp, 2019 —
  [basecamp.com/shapeup](https://basecamp.com/shapeup). The canonical
  source for the six-week-appetite / two-week-cool-down shape, the
  shaping / betting / building / hill-chart vocabulary, and the
  descope-to-fit discipline used in Lecture 2 and
  [Exercise 2](exercises/exercise-02-shape-up-vs-weekly-kanban-choice-drill.md).
  Chapters 1–3 are the pitch / shaping anatomy; Chapters 8–12 are the
  bet-meeting and cycle-execution mechanics; Chapter 13 (hills) is the
  reference for the in-flight review Lecture 6 names as the design-
  debt-in-review counter-move.
- David J. Anderson, *Kanban: Successful Evolutionary Change for Your
  Technology Business*, Blue Hole Press, 2010 —
  [leankanban.com/kanban-book](http://leankanban.com/kanban-book/).
  The canonical statement of the kanban discipline — visualize the
  work, limit WIP, manage flow, make policies explicit — Lecture 2 and
  Exercise 2 lean on for the weekly-ship-kanban operating model.
- Don Reinertsen, *The Principles of Product Development Flow: Second
  Generation Lean Product Development*, Celeritas, 2009 —
  [celeritaspublishing.com](https://celeritaspublishing.com/product/the-principles-of-product-development-flow/).
  Chapters 3 and 5 supply the queueing-theory arguments for small-
  batch commitments, WIP limits, and the Cost of Delay math that
  underpin Lecture 2's weekly-kanban discipline and Lecture 6's
  argument against infrequent-large commitment rituals.
- Henrik Kniberg & Mattias Skarin, *Kanban and Scrum: Making the Most
  of Both*, InfoQ, 2010 —
  [infoq.com/minibooks/kanban-scrum-minibook](https://www.infoq.com/minibooks/kanban-scrum-minibook/).
  A single-sitting practitioner contrast of kanban and Scrum that
  motivates Lecture 2's refusal of Scrum-at-startup-scale; useful for
  readers who need the compare-and-contrast before committing to
  weekly-kanban.

## PRD form — Amazon six-pager / PR-FAQ (Bezos, Bryar & Carr, Perri)

- Jeff Bezos, "2004 Letter to Shareholders," Amazon, 2005 —
  [aboutamazon.com/news/company-news/2004-letter-to-shareholders](https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/company-news/2004-letter-to-shareholders).
  The original public mandate for the narrative six-pager and the
  prohibition on PowerPoint for senior review; cited in
  [Lecture 3](lectures/03-prd-in-amazon-6-pager-shape.md) and
  [Lecture 6](lectures/06-ship-velocity-anti-patterns.md) for the
  anti-PRD-gestation discipline.
- Colin Bryar & Bill Carr, *Working Backwards: Insights, Stories, and
  Secrets from Inside Amazon*, St. Martin's Press, 2021 —
  [workingbackwards.com](https://www.workingbackwards.com/). Chapter
  4 ("Narratives and the Six-Pager") is the source for the narrative-
  vs-bullet-points argument and the silent-meeting reading ritual.
  Chapter 5 ("Working Backwards") is the source for the PR/FAQ
  variant, the mock-press-release-first discipline, and the internal
  / external FAQ structures used in Lecture 3 and
  [Exercise 3](exercises/exercise-03-prd-in-amazon-6-pager-shape.md).
- Melissa Perri, *Escaping the Build Trap: How Effective Product
  Management Creates Real Value*, O'Reilly, 2018 —
  [oreilly.com](https://www.oreilly.com/library/view/escaping-the-build/9781491973783/).
  The outcome-shaped-proposal discipline that Lecture 3 relies on for
  the general-six-pager "proposal" section; already cited at depth in
  [mod-003 resources.md](../mod-003-roadmap-outcomes-and-strategy/resources.md).

## Design, affordances, hierarchy (Norman, Krug, Lupton, Basecamp)

- Don Norman, *The Design of Everyday Things* (revised and expanded),
  Basic Books, 2013 —
  [mitpress.mit.edu/9780262525671](https://mitpress.mit.edu/9780262525671/the-design-of-everyday-things/).
  Chapter 1 is the source of the affordance / signifier distinction
  that
  [Lecture 4](lectures/04-own-the-pixel-figma-and-microcopy.md)'s
  Discipline 1 ("affordance test") rests on, and that
  [Exercise 4](exercises/exercise-04-figma-mockup-and-microcopy-drill.md)'s
  pre-ship walk tests directly.
- Steve Krug, *Don't Make Me Think, Revisited: A Common Sense Approach
  to Web Usability*, New Riders, 2014 —
  [sensible.com/dmmt.html](https://www.sensible.com/dmmt.html). The
  five-second-test and visual-hierarchy framings Lecture 4
  Discipline 2 uses at CPO scope.
- Ellen Lupton, *Thinking with Type: A Critical Guide for Designers,
  Writers, Editors, & Students* (revised, 2nd ed.), Princeton
  Architectural Press, 2010 —
  [papress.com/thinking-with-type](https://www.papress.com/products/thinking-with-type).
  Reference for the typography half of visual hierarchy; a CPO-
  appropriate read for the hierarchy-is-a-thinking-tool discipline
  in Lecture 4.
- Basecamp (37signals), *Getting Real: The Smarter, Faster, Easier
  Way to Build a Successful Web Application*, 37signals, 2006 —
  [basecamp.com/gettingreal](https://basecamp.com/gettingreal). The
  "Copywriting is interface design" chapter is the historical source
  for Lecture 4's "empty state is the product" discipline and for
  the live-changelog form that Linear, Notion, and Basecamp itself
  now model.

## Microcopy, voice, and tone (Yifrah, Mailchimp, GOV.UK)

- Kinneret Yifrah, *Microcopy: The Complete Guide*, self-published,
  2017 —
  [microcopybook.com](https://www.microcopybook.com/). The canonical
  practitioner treatment of button labels, error messages, empty
  states, and tone-as-decision used in Lecture 4 and Exercise 4.
- Mailchimp, *Content Style Guide* —
  [styleguide.mailchimp.com](https://styleguide.mailchimp.com/).
  Working example of a product-wide voice / tone guide at the shape
  Lecture 4 recommends a founding CPO draft. The *Voice and tone*
  and *Writing goals and principles* sections are the direct
  references.
- GOV.UK, *Design System — Content guidance* —
  [design-system.service.gov.uk/styles/content](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/styles/content/).
  A plain-language counterpart to Mailchimp — more terse, more
  precise, equally applicable. Useful when the Mailchimp tone is
  too warm for your product.

## DORA metrics and the CTO-side cadence (Forsgren, Humble & Kim, Google, Majors)

- Nicole Forsgren, Jez Humble & Gene Kim, *Accelerate: The Science
  of Lean Software and DevOps*, IT Revolution, 2018 —
  [itrevolution.com/product/accelerate](https://itrevolution.com/product/accelerate/).
  The canonical reference for the four DORA metrics (deploy
  frequency, lead time for changes, change-failure rate, mean time
  to restore) and the research methodology behind them; Lecture 2
  and Lecture 6 both cite this as the measurement regime the CPO
  consumes.
- Google Cloud, *DORA — DevOps Research and Assessment* —
  [dora.dev](https://dora.dev/). The ongoing program maintaining
  the metrics, the annual *State of DevOps* report, and the
  current operational definitions used in Lecture 6 and
  [Exercise 6](exercises/exercise-06-ship-velocity-anti-pattern-diagnosis.md).
- Betsy Beyer, Chris Jones, Jennifer Petoff & Niall Richard
  Murphy (eds.), *Site Reliability Engineering: How Google Runs
  Production Systems*, O'Reilly, 2016 — read online at
  [sre.google/sre-book](https://sre.google/sre-book/table-of-contents/).
  The canonical reference for the SRE regime that produces the
  DORA numbers Lecture 6 asks the CPO to consume. SRE authorship
  itself is level-25 work owned by
  [cto-curriculum](https://github.com/ai-startup-curriculum/cto-curriculum).
- Charity Majors, Liz Fong-Jones & George Miranda,
  *Observability Engineering: Achieving Production Excellence*,
  O'Reilly, 2022 —
  [oreilly.com/library/view/observability-engineering/9781492076438](https://www.oreilly.com/library/view/observability-engineering/9781492076438/).
  The modern practitioner reference for the observability side of
  the SRE regime; cited in Lecture 6 as part of the level-25
  boundary.

## Adjacent reading (light touch)

The following are not cited in the module body but are one-step
neighbors that show up in most founding-CPO reading lists on cadence
and delivery craft:

- Lenny Rachitsky, "How the best product teams ship," *Lenny's
  Newsletter* — [lennysnewsletter.com](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/).
  Practitioner survey material on cadence choices at seed to
  Series-C stage; useful as a sanity-check after Singer and
  Anderson.
- Shreyas Doshi, *Shreyas Doshi on PM craft* — the
  @shreyas thread corpus and newsletter at
  [shreyasdoshi.substack.com](https://shreyasdoshi.substack.com/).
  Practitioner commentary on PRD authoring, cadence, and the "LNO"
  framing (leverage / neutral / overhead work) adjacent to Lecture
  1's CPO-time-budget discipline.
- John Cutler, *The Beautiful Mess*, newsletter —
  [cutlefish.substack.com](https://cutlefish.substack.com/).
  Running practitioner commentary on cadence anti-patterns; useful
  as a continuous diagnostic read after Lecture 6.
- Allen Holub, "The Death of Agile," *youtube.com* and essays at
  [holub.com](https://www.holub.com/). Polemic against the
  degenerate forms of Scrum that Lecture 2 and Lecture 6 refuse;
  useful as a sharper version of the argument.
- Ryan Singer, *Felt Time* and the Basecamp "Hills" essays at
  [feltpresence.com](https://feltpresence.com/). Singer's
  post-Basecamp commentary extending the Shape Up framing;
  recommended once *Shape Up* itself has been read.
- Teresa Torres, blog archive at
  [producttalk.org](https://www.producttalk.org/). Running
  practitioner commentary on continuous discovery with longer-form
  essays on the opportunity solution tree, the weekly interview,
  and the trio.
- Julie Zhuo, *The Looking Glass* / *The Making of a Manager*
  companion newsletter —
  [lg.substack.com](https://lg.substack.com/). Design-leader
  commentary adjacent to Lecture 4's "own the pixel" discipline;
  useful once Krug and Norman have been read.
- Mitchell Hashimoto, "Developer tool product writing," personal
  essays at [mitchellh.com](https://mitchellh.com/writing). The
  developer-tool-adjacent slice of microcopy and changelog
  discipline; useful if your product's surface is developer-
  facing.

If a claim in the module cites a source not listed here, treat it as
a `needs-research` gap and open an issue.
