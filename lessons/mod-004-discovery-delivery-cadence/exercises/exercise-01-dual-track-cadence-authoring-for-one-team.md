# Exercise 1 — Dual-track cadence authoring for one team

**Time:** ~3 hours. **Deliverable:** one dual-track cadence
artifact — team-shape memo, weekly calendar, discovery
rhythm, delivery rhythm, seam rituals, anti-drift rules —
for a real (or plausible synthetic) team, plus a
reviewer's memo on what would break first.

## Purpose

Author the **weekly operating rhythm** described in
[Lecture 1](../lectures/01-dual-track-discovery-delivery-cadence.md) —
Marty Cagan's dual-track model plus Teresa Torres's
continuous-discovery weekly cadence — for a specific team
you name. The cadence artifact is the input every other
exercise in this module depends on; don't skip to Exercise
2 without a rhythm you can defend.

## Choosing the team

Use one team you can name concretely. In order of
preference:

1. **The product team at the company you're currently
   working at (or about to join).** The rhythm work is
   real.
2. **A team you recently ran or observed closely enough
   that you remember the day-of-week shape.**
3. **A plausible synthetic** — an AI-native workflow team
   of one CPO, one designer (or CPO wearing that hat),
   and 3–5 engineers, at a pre-seed / seed-stage company
   you name. If you go synthetic, write half a page up
   front naming: current headcount, current-quarter
   discovery maturity (weekly interviews already
   happening or not), current delivery-operating-model
   (or absence of one), primary product surface, and
   whether the surface is AI-substrate-heavy. The
   cadence cannot be judged in the absence of them.

Whichever you pick, the artifact is authored *as if you
were the founding CPO today* — not as a case study.

## What the cadence artifact must contain

Four to six pages. Adopt the shape from
[Lecture 1](../lectures/01-dual-track-discovery-delivery-cadence.md),
in the following order:

### 1. Team-shape memo (half page)

- **The trio you have (or approximate).** Name the PM
  (or the CPO wearing that hat), the product designer
  (or the CPO wearing that hat), and the tech lead. Which
  of the three shapes from
  [Lecture 1](../lectures/01-dual-track-discovery-delivery-cadence.md#what-the-founding-cpo-does-personally)
  are you closest to?
- **The engineering team.** How many engineers total,
  their seniority mix, and any specialization (backend,
  frontend, ML) that will shape the delivery rhythm.
- **Reporting shape.** Does design report to product, to
  eng, to the CPO, or to nobody yet? Does the tech lead
  report to the CTO or the CPO? Name it; it shapes what
  the trio's Thursday sync can actually decide.
- **Discovery maturity today.** Are weekly interviews
  already happening? Is there an opportunity tree? Is
  Cagan's *four risks* framing (value / usability /
  feasibility / viability) in the trio's vocabulary?

### 2. Weekly calendar (one page)

A day-by-day layout of the rhythm. Concrete: what
happens on Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday, Thursday, Friday.
For each ritual on the calendar:

- **Name of the ritual.** Match the Lecture 1
  vocabulary where possible: weekly interview /
  touchpoint, assumption tests, Thursday discovery /
  delivery sync, Friday shipped-slice review.
- **Attendees.** Which subset of the trio (and, if
  needed, the wider team).
- **Duration.** 30 min / 45 min / 60 min. Be honest —
  a ritual that's really 15 minutes should not be on
  the calendar as 60.
- **Output.** What artifact does the ritual produce?
  ("Two new opportunity nodes on the tree" / "one
  assumption falsified" / "one graduation from
  discovery to delivery" / "one shipped-slice review
  entry.") A ritual without an output is a meeting;
  don't put meetings on the cadence.
- **Owner.** Whose job it is to run it and to publish
  the output.

Adapt the day-of-week naming to your team's reality —
some teams do the sync Friday, some do it Wednesday.
Justify your choice.

### 3. Discovery rhythm (half page)

- **Weekly interview / touchpoint discipline.** Who is
  in the room. What kinds of customer / user (segment,
  role, lifecycle stage). How you'll source them
  weekly — recruiting is where discovery rhythms
  usually die.
- **Assumption-test cadence.** How many assumption
  tests per week, on what shape (prototype, fake-door,
  data pull, unmoderated usability, eval sweep for
  AI-substrate features). Who runs them.
- **The opportunity solution tree.** Where it lives
  (Miro, FigJam, a specific tool). Who edits it. How
  often it gets pruned. How dormant opportunities are
  aged out.

### 4. Delivery rhythm (half page — placeholder)

You will pick the delivery operating model in
[Exercise 2](exercise-02-shape-up-vs-weekly-kanban-choice-drill.md).
For this exercise, leave a *placeholder*: a paragraph
naming which model you *think* you'll pick, why (using
the Lecture 2 six signals at surface level), and what
the weekly-ship or per-cycle rhythm of the delivery
track *would* look like at that shape. Return to this
section after Exercise 2 and refine.

### 5. The seam — how discovery informs delivery (half page)

- **Thursday sync agenda.** The specific three-question
  agenda from
  [Lecture 1](../lectures/01-dual-track-discovery-delivery-cadence.md#thursday--the-trios-discovery--delivery-sync):
  what did discovery learn, what does delivery need
  next, what does next week's queue look like.
- **The pre-registered-signal discipline.** How you'll
  make sure every graduated opportunity gets a metric
  and horizon at graduation time. Where those signals
  live (in the opportunity tree, in the six-pager, in
  the roadmap tool).
- **The traceability rule.** How you'll enforce that
  every delivery slice traces back to an opportunity
  on the tree.

### 6. Anti-drift rules (quarter page)

Name at least three failure modes from
[Lecture 1](../lectures/01-dual-track-discovery-delivery-cadence.md#where-dual-track-fails)
your team is most likely to fall into (discovery drift,
track detachment, trio collapse), and for each: the
*structural* counter-move you're building into the
cadence to prevent it. Not "we'll try harder"; a specific
rule (e.g., "Friday review is publicly cancelled if the
Monday interview was skipped").

### 7. CPO time budget (quarter page)

Roll up the calendar into how many hours per week the
CPO spends on this rhythm. Ideally 8–12 hours (Monday
interview, assumption test co-design, Thursday sync,
Friday review, opportunity-tree tending). If your
calendar has the CPO at 25 hours, the rhythm is
CPO-bottlenecked and won't survive; go back and cut.

## Starter guidance

- **Author the discovery rhythm first.** The delivery
  rhythm is Exercise 2's job. Discovery is the harder
  half to get right at pre-seed / seed scale because
  it usually doesn't already exist and has to be
  built.
- **The weekly interview is the load-bearing ritual.**
  If it's not on the calendar as recurring, owned by
  the CPO, with a real recruiting mechanism behind it,
  the cadence will silently degrade to delivery-only
  in eight weeks.
- **Name the trio explicitly, even if you're wearing
  two hats.** The trio is a structural discipline; a
  vague "we all work on it together" is where trio
  collapse hides.
- **Don't put meetings on the cadence.** Every ritual
  produces an artifact. A "product sync" without a
  named output is a meeting; delete it or give it an
  output.
- **The Thursday sync is the seam.** If you don't have
  one, the two tracks will detach within a month.
  Don't skip it because "we already talk enough."

## The reviewer's memo

Half a page, written after the cadence artifact.
Answer:

- **What would break first?** If you had to bet on which
  ritual will silently degrade in month three, which
  one is it? Why? What in the cadence design would
  prevent it?
- **CPO time reality check.** Add up the CPO's cadence
  hours. What are you *not* doing with the remaining
  hours — are they realistic for the founding-CPO job
  at your stage?
- **Comparison to today.** How does the cadence you
  just authored differ from the way this team currently
  operates (if a real team) or from the default a
  first-time CPO would produce (if synthetic)? Which
  single change is likely to face the most resistance?

## Acceptance criteria

- Team-shape memo names the trio, engineering team,
  reporting shape, and discovery maturity.
- Weekly calendar has at least four named rituals with
  attendees / duration / output / owner.
- Discovery rhythm includes weekly interview
  discipline, an assumption-test cadence, and an
  opportunity-tree location + owner.
- Delivery rhythm placeholder names a probable
  operating model and cites the Lecture 2 signals
  driving the pick.
- Seam section names the Thursday sync agenda, the
  pre-registered-signal discipline, and the
  traceability rule.
- At least three anti-drift rules are named, each with
  a structural counter-move (not "we'll try harder").
- CPO time budget adds up and stays under 15 hours per
  week on this rhythm alone.
- Reviewer's memo answers all three prompts.

## Common failure modes

- **Discovery-as-optional.** The calendar has the
  Monday interview as "if we can" rather than as a
  hard weekly commitment. This is the single most
  common failure; the cadence collapses within two
  months.
- **Meeting-shaped rituals.** Rituals without a named
  output. If you can't say what artifact the ritual
  produces, it's a meeting, not a cadence ritual.
- **Trio-invisible.** The document talks about "the
  team" without naming which three people form the
  trio for a given bet. Trio collapse hides here.
- **CPO-as-everything.** The CPO is on every ritual,
  every day, for a total of 30 CPO-hours. That's not
  a cadence; that's a bottleneck.
- **Seam-as-status-meeting.** The Thursday sync is
  described as "we go around the room and share
  updates." That's a status meeting. The sync has to
  produce a *decision* (graduate this opportunity,
  descope this slice, kill this bet), not a report.
- **Anti-drift-as-culture.** The anti-drift rules read
  as "we'll be disciplined" rather than as structural
  rules the cadence enforces mechanically.

## Source alignment

The dual-track shape derives from Marty Cagan,
*Inspired* (2nd ed.), Wiley, 2017, Part IV: "The Right
Process" —
[svpg.com/inspired](https://www.svpg.com/inspired-how-to-create-products-customers-love/);
and Marty Cagan with Chris Jones, *Empowered*, Wiley,
2020, Chapters 8–10 —
[svpg.com/empowered](https://www.svpg.com/empowered-ordinary-people-extraordinary-products/).
The weekly cadence and continuous-discovery vocabulary
derive from Teresa Torres, *Continuous Discovery
Habits*, Product Talk, 2021 —
[producttalk.org/continuous-discovery-habits](https://www.producttalk.org/continuous-discovery-habits/).
The dual-track term itself was named by Desirée Sy in
2007 and generalized by Jeff Patton in 2017 — see
[uxpajournal.org, Adapting Usability Investigations for
Agile User-Centered Design](https://uxpajournal.org/adapting-usability-investigations-for-agile-user-centered-design/)
and
[jpattonassociates.com/dual-track-development](https://www.jpattonassociates.com/dual-track-development/).
