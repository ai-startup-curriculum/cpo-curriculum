# mod-003 — Roadmap Craft: Outcomes over Output, Multi-Quarter Strategy

> The founding CPO's third job — after finding something someone will
> pull out of your hands (mod-001) and ranking the queue that lets you
> pick the next bet (mod-002) — is authoring a **multi-quarter product
> strategy** and a roadmap a first-week hire can read. The roadmap is
> the CPO's single most-consumed deliverable.

## Why this module exists

Ask a first-time CPO for their roadmap and you will usually get a
quarter-by-quarter feature calendar. Ask three different audiences —
the board, GTM, the engineering team — for their reading of the same
document and you will get three different, mutually-incompatible
stories. Ask what the *strategy* is that generated it and you will
often get either a slogan or a market-size chart. This is the shape
of the failure this module is written against.

A founding CPO who cannot author a strategy — in the Rumelt-shape,
diagnosis / guiding-policy / coherent-action sense — is a CPO whose
prioritization queue silently drifts into whatever the loudest
stakeholder asks for that week. A founding CPO who cannot express
that strategy as an **outcomes-based roadmap** — in the Perri /
build-trap sense — is a CPO whose team keeps shipping features it
cannot connect to a moving business number. A founding CPO who
authors one roadmap document and hands it to three audiences is a
CPO who gets misread by two of them. And a founding CPO who cannot
disagree with a founder-CEO in writing — cleanly, on the record,
with a defined tie-breaker — is a CPO who becomes a Jira admin.

This module teaches the strategy craft, the roadmap shape, the OKRs
that declare outcomes without micromanaging teams, the platform-vs-
point-product bet, the three-audience communication discipline, and
the founder-CEO disagreement pattern that lets you author roadmaps
you can defend and change roadmaps you have to.

## Learning outcomes

By the end of this module you can:

1. **Author** a multi-quarter product strategy that satisfies Rumelt's
   diagnosis-guiding-policy-coherent-action test and Perri's
   escape-the-build-trap shape — one a first-week hire can read to
   understand what the company is and is not doing.
2. **Design** an outcomes-based roadmap (Now / Next / Later, or
   objective-oriented) that names desired customer / business
   outcomes rather than feature shipments, and route product OKRs at
   the CPO scope as an outcome-declaration tool rather than a Jira
   surrogate.
3. **Reason** about platform / API-first / multi-surface strategy —
   Thompson's aggregation-theory frame, Choudary's platform-scaling
   frame, Winters's product-strategy essays — well enough to defend
   where the platform bet is, where the point-product bet is, and
   what would falsify each.
4. **Communicate** the same roadmap up (board / CEO), sideways (GTM
   / eng / design), and down (team) in three separately-authored
   artifacts, each written for what its reader must do differently
   after they read it.
5. **Handle** roadmap disagreement with a founder-CEO — decision
   rights, tie-breaker, disagree-and-commit — in the Alex-AI-shaped
   flat structure a first-time founding CPO actually operates in.
6. **Consume** company-strategy authoring at the level-20 boundary
   (owned by
   [founder-ceo-curriculum](https://github.com/ai-startup-curriculum/founder-ceo-curriculum)),
   board-facing narrative craft at the level-40 boundary (owned by
   [startup-finance-fundraising-curriculum](https://github.com/ai-startup-curriculum/startup-finance-fundraising-curriculum)),
   and architectural roadmap coordination at the level-25 boundary
   (owned by
   [cto-curriculum](https://github.com/ai-startup-curriculum/cto-curriculum)).

## How to work this module

Read the lectures in order. Lecture 1 gives you the shape a strategy
has to have before anyone should be reading a roadmap; Lecture 2
turns that strategy into a Now / Next / Later or objective-oriented
roadmap shape; Lecture 3 introduces OKRs at *the CPO's* scope, not
the team's; Lecture 4 pulls up to the platform / point-product bet
that decides which surface the strategy runs on; Lecture 5 gets you
three read-outs of the same roadmap for three different readers; and
Lecture 6 closes on the disagreement pattern that lets a founding
CPO stay in the job when they and the founder-CEO see it
differently.

Do the exercises against a real product. The failure modes this
module teaches — a strategy that is a slogan, a roadmap that is a
calendar, OKRs that are Jira tickets, a platform bet with no
falsifier, a board deck read to the team, a founder-CEO
disagreement handled by capitulation — are almost invisible in toy
cases. Use the same product you took through mod-001 and mod-002 if
you can.

Exercises 1 → 6 build on each other. Exercise 1 produces the
strategy Exercise 2 turns into a roadmap and Exercise 3 declares
OKRs against; Exercise 4 defends the platform-vs-point-product bet
underneath both; Exercise 5 produces the three-audience read-outs;
Exercise 6 forces the whole thing through a founder-CEO
disagreement so you feel where the discipline pinches.

## Deliverables

- One multi-quarter product strategy in Rumelt-shape (diagnosis,
  guiding policy, coherent actions) plus a reviewer's memo
  ([Exercise 1](exercises/exercise-01-rumelt-diagnosis-guiding-policy-coherent-action-drill.md)).
- One outcomes-based roadmap in Now / Next / Later shape (or the
  objective-oriented variant) with named outcomes, evidence, and
  falsifiers
  ([Exercise 2](exercises/exercise-02-outcomes-based-roadmap-authoring.md)).
- Three CPO-scope OKRs plus a scope-boundary memo naming what they
  are and are not for
  ([Exercise 3](exercises/exercise-03-okrs-at-cpo-scope-drill.md)).
- A platform-vs-point-product bet defense with an aggregation-theory
  read, a platform-scaling read, and a pre-registered falsifier
  ([Exercise 4](exercises/exercise-04-platform-vs-point-product-bet-drill.md)).
- Three separately-authored roadmap read-outs — one for the board /
  CEO, one for GTM / eng / design, one for the team — plus a
  cross-artifact consistency check
  ([Exercise 5](exercises/exercise-05-roadmap-communication-in-three-audiences.md)).
- A written disagreement memo with a founder-CEO on one real
  roadmap call, including decision-rights annotation and a
  disagree-and-commit close
  ([Exercise 6](exercises/exercise-06-founder-ceo-disagreement-decision-drill.md)).

## Boundaries this module keeps

- **Discovery craft** — hypotheses, interviews, JTBD synthesis,
  PMF measurement — is owned by
  [mod-001](../mod-001-customer-discovery-to-pmf/README.md). This
  module consumes the opportunity nodes discovery produces; it does
  not re-teach the discovery loop.
- **Ranking and killing** — RICE / WSJF / Kano, multi-surface
  allocation, quarterly kill review — is owned by
  [mod-002](../mod-002-opportunity-assessment-and-prioritization/README.md).
  This module consumes the ranked portfolio; it does not re-rank.
- **Delivery cadence and PRD craft** — dual-track rhythm, Shape Up
  vs weekly kanban, six-pager PRDs, agentic-UX iteration loops —
  are the subject of
  [mod-004](../mod-004-discovery-delivery-cadence/README.md). The
  roadmap tells delivery what outcomes to move; delivery decides
  how.
- **Metrics, experiments, evals** — the instrumentation that makes
  an outcomes roadmap measurable — are the subject of
  [mod-005](../mod-005-metrics-experimentation-and-ai-evals/README.md).
  OKR key results assume that instrumentation exists.
- **Pricing and packaging** — including the pricing surface that
  often dominates the roadmap under a tight runway — is the
  subject of
  [mod-006](../mod-006-pricing-packaging-and-monetization/README.md).
- **The founder-CEO / eng-lead / GTM-lead relationship contract**
  underneath the disagreement mechanics is the subject of
  [mod-007](../mod-007-working-with-founders-eng-and-gtm/README.md).
  This module teaches the roadmap-specific disagreement pattern;
  mod-007 teaches the general relationship it lives inside.
- **Company-strategy authoring** — the Rumelt-shape *company*
  strategy the *product* strategy is a subsection of — is level-20
  work owned by
  [founder-ceo-curriculum](https://github.com/ai-startup-curriculum/founder-ceo-curriculum).
  The CPO consumes the company strategy; they do not author it
  alone.
- **Board-facing narrative craft** — the fundraising / narrative /
  story-of-the-company polish that the board read-out lives inside
  — is level-40 work owned by
  [startup-finance-fundraising-curriculum](https://github.com/ai-startup-curriculum/startup-finance-fundraising-curriculum).
  This module teaches the *product-strategy content* of the board
  read-out; that curriculum teaches the wrapper.
- **Architectural roadmap coordination** — the sequencing of
  platform, API, and data-model bets in engineering time — is
  level-25 work owned by
  [cto-curriculum](https://github.com/ai-startup-curriculum/cto-curriculum).
  Lecture 4 gives the CPO the vocabulary to have that conversation;
  it does not teach the CTO's side of it.
