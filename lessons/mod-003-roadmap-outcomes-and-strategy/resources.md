# Resources — mod-003 Roadmap Craft: Outcomes over Output, Multi-Quarter Strategy

Primary sources for the material in this module. Prefer the books over
the blog posts for depth; prefer the essays and HBR articles when you
need a single-sitting refresher. Every URL below is either the author's
canonical page, the publisher's page, or an archived copy where the
original has moved or lapsed.

## Strategy kernel and the product-strategy stack (Rumelt, Perri, Winters)

- Richard Rumelt, *Good Strategy Bad Strategy: The Difference and Why
  It Matters*, Crown Business, 2011 —
  [crownpublishing.com](https://www.crownpublishing.com/archives/feature/good-strategy-bad-strategy-richard-rumelt).
  Chapter 5, "The Kernel of Good Strategy," is the source for the
  diagnosis / guiding-policy / coherent-action shape used throughout
  [Lecture 1](lectures/01-rumelt-shape-strategy-for-the-cpo.md) and
  [Exercise 1](exercises/exercise-01-rumelt-diagnosis-guiding-policy-coherent-action-drill.md).
  Chapters 2–4 catalogue the four bad-strategy hallmarks (fluff,
  failure to face the challenge, mistaking goals for strategy, bad
  strategic objectives) cited in the lecture.
- Melissa Perri, *Escaping the Build Trap: How Effective Product
  Management Creates Real Value*, O'Reilly, 2018 —
  [oreilly.com](https://www.oreilly.com/library/view/escaping-the-build/9781491973783/).
  Chapter 3, "The Build Trap and What It Costs," is the source for the
  output / outcome distinction that supplies the *content* of the
  Rumelt kernel in Lecture 1. Chapter 4, "The Product Strategy Gap,"
  is the lecture-1 reference for why the kernel has to be authored at
  the product scope. Chapters 7–9 are the direct references for
  Lecture 2's outcomes-based-roadmap discipline.
- Casey Winters, "The Product Strategy Stack," *caseyaccidental.com*,
  2019 —
  [caseyaccidental.com/the-product-strategy-stack](https://caseyaccidental.com/the-product-strategy-stack/).
  The layered decomposition — company vision → company strategy →
  product vision → product strategy → product initiatives → team
  objectives — used in [Lecture 1](lectures/01-rumelt-shape-strategy-for-the-cpo.md)
  to locate the CPO's authoring scope and in
  [Lecture 4](lectures/04-platform-vs-point-product-bet.md) to locate
  the platform-vs-point-product choice inside the stack.

## Outcomes-based roadmaps (Bastow, Cagan, Perri)

- Janna Bastow, "The Now / Next / Later Roadmap," ProdPad, 2013
  onward — [prodpad.com/blog/tag/now-next-later](https://www.prodpad.com/blog/tag/now-next-later/).
  Canonical statement of the three-column outcomes-shaped roadmap and
  the *roadmap-as-conversation-prompt* framing used in
  [Lecture 2](lectures/02-outcomes-based-roadmap-shape.md) and
  [Exercise 2](exercises/exercise-02-outcomes-based-roadmap-authoring.md).
- Marty Cagan, *Empowered: Ordinary People, Extraordinary Products*,
  Wiley, 2020 —
  [svpg.com/empowered](https://www.svpg.com/empowered-ordinary-people-extraordinary-products/).
  Chapter 25 is the reference for the objective-oriented roadmap
  shape — teams accountable for product outcomes rather than feature
  shipments — cited in Lecture 2 and Exercise 2. Chapters 25–27 cover
  the product-vision / insights / principles layer the three-audience
  read-out depends on in Lecture 5.
- Melissa Perri, *Escaping the Build Trap*, O'Reilly, 2018 (as above)
  — Chapters 7–9 give the outcome-vs-output discipline the Now / Next
  / Later and objective-oriented roadmaps both operationalize.

## OKRs at the CPO scope (Grove, Doerr, Wodtke)

- Andrew Grove, *High Output Management*, Vintage, 1983 / 1995 —
  [penguinrandomhouse.com](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/158477/high-output-management-by-andrew-s-grove/).
  Where the OKR mechanic originates at mid-1970s Intel; the
  references for the *leverage* framing cited in
  [Lecture 3](lectures/03-okrs-at-cpo-scope.md) and
  [Exercise 3](exercises/exercise-03-okrs-at-cpo-scope-drill.md).
- John Doerr, *Measure What Matters: How Google, Bono, and the Gates
  Foundation Rock the World with OKRs*, Portfolio, 2018 —
  [whatmatters.com](https://www.whatmatters.com/the-book). Chapter 1
  for the Objective-vs-Key-Result shape; the full catalogue of
  real-company OKR examples cited in Lecture 3.
- Christina Wodtke, *Radical Focus: Achieving Your Most Important
  Goals with Objectives and Key Results* (2nd ed.), Cucina Media,
  2021 —
  [eleganthack.com/the-art-of-the-okr](https://eleganthack.com/the-art-of-the-okr/).
  The 0.7-target norm, the committed / aspirational KR split,
  mid-quarter confidence rituals, and the anti-cascade discipline
  used in Lecture 3 and Exercise 3.

## Platform, API, aggregation, and multi-surface strategy (Thompson, Choudary, Van Alstyne)

- Ben Thompson, "Aggregation Theory," *Stratechery*, July 2015 —
  [stratechery.com/2015/aggregation-theory](https://stratechery.com/2015/aggregation-theory/).
  The framing used in
  [Lecture 4](lectures/04-platform-vs-point-product-bet.md) and
  [Exercise 4](exercises/exercise-04-platform-vs-point-product-bet-drill.md)
  for reasoning about where value concentrates as a market matures
  and whether your surface is in a position to aggregate.
- Ben Thompson, "Defining Aggregators," *Stratechery*, February
  2017 —
  [stratechery.com/2017/defining-aggregators](https://stratechery.com/2017/defining-aggregators/).
  The three-property refinement — direct user relationship, zero
  marginal cost per user, decreasing acquisition cost on
  multi-sided demand — used as the diagnostic in Lecture 4.
- Sangeet Paul Choudary, *Platform Scale: How an Emerging Business
  Model Helps Startups Build Large Empires with Minimum Investment*,
  Platform Thinking Labs, 2015 —
  [platformed.info/books](https://platformed.info/books/). Source
  for the chicken-and-egg / initial-side framing, the cross-side-vs-
  same-side network-effect distinction, and the "fund the scarce
  side" heuristic in Lecture 4 and Exercise 4.
- Marshall Van Alstyne, Geoffrey Parker & Sangeet Paul Choudary,
  "Pipelines, Platforms, and the New Rules of Strategy," *Harvard
  Business Review*, April 2016 —
  [hbr.org/2016/04/pipelines-platforms-and-the-new-rules-of-strategy](https://hbr.org/2016/04/pipelines-platforms-and-the-new-rules-of-strategy).
  Single-sitting summary of the platform-strategy argument. Used in
  Lecture 4 for the pipeline-in-platform-costume warning.
- Casey Winters, "The Product Strategy Stack," 2019 (as above) —
  the strategy-stack lens for the platform-vs-point-product choice
  in Lecture 4.

## Adjacent reading (light touch)

The following are not cited in the module but are one-step neighbors
that show up in most founding-CPO reading lists on roadmap and
strategy craft:

- Michael E. Porter, "What Is Strategy?," *Harvard Business Review*,
  November–December 1996 —
  [hbr.org/1996/11/what-is-strategy](https://hbr.org/1996/11/what-is-strategy).
  Porter's *strategy is choosing what not to do* argument, a useful
  one-sitting companion to Rumelt's *good strategy discards*
  discipline from Lecture 1.
- Roger L. Martin, *Playing to Win: How Strategy Really Works*,
  Harvard Business Review Press, 2013 —
  [rogermartin.com](https://rogermartin.com/books/playing-to-win/).
  The *where-to-play / how-to-win* cascade; a useful companion
  framing when authoring the guiding policy in Exercise 1.
- Teresa Torres, *Continuous Discovery Habits*, Product Talk, 2021 —
  [producttalk.org/continuous-discovery-habits](https://www.producttalk.org/continuous-discovery-habits/).
  Already cited in mod-001; the Chapter 6 "opportunity solution
  tree" is the direct feeder to the outcomes that fill a Lecture-2
  roadmap.
- Shishir Mehrotra, "The Art of the OKR," *Coda Blog*, 2020 —
  [coda.io/@shishir/the-art-of-the-okr](https://coda.io/@shishir/the-art-of-the-okr).
  Practitioner catalogue of OKR anti-patterns at YouTube and Coda;
  a useful sanity-check against Wodtke's framing from Lecture 3.
