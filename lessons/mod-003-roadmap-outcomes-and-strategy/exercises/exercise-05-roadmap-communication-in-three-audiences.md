# Exercise 5 — Roadmap communication in three audiences

**Time:** ~3 hours. **Deliverable:** three separately-authored
roadmap read-outs — one for the board / CEO, one for a peer
head (pick GTM, engineering, or design), one for a product
team — plus a cross-artifact consistency-check memo.

## Purpose

Take the strategy from
[Exercise 1](exercise-01-rumelt-diagnosis-guiding-policy-coherent-action-drill.md),
the roadmap from
[Exercise 2](exercise-02-outcomes-based-roadmap-authoring.md),
the OKRs from
[Exercise 3](exercise-03-okrs-at-cpo-scope-drill.md), and the
platform bet from
[Exercise 4](exercise-04-platform-vs-point-product-bet-drill.md),
and translate them into **three distinct read-outs**, each
tuned for what its reader must do differently after reading —
per the discipline in
[Lecture 5](../lectures/05-three-audience-roadmap-communication.md).
The consistency check is where you catch the discrepancies
between the three artifacts before publication.

## What you need before you start

- Exercises 1 through 4 complete. The three read-outs are
  *translations* of the source-of-truth artifacts; if the
  source of truth isn't tight, the read-outs will not be
  either.
- A picked peer function — **GTM, engineering, or design**
  — for the peer read-out. You will author one, not all
  three. Pick the peer whose alignment on this strategy
  most matters at your company.

## Read-out one — for the board and founder-CEO (2–3 pages or 6–8 slides)

Written memo or slide deck with speaker notes. Follow the
six-section shape in
[Lecture 5](../lectures/05-three-audience-roadmap-communication.md#read-out-one--for-the-board-and-founder-ceo):

1. **Strategic diagnosis and how it has changed.** One
   paragraph. Reference the diagnosis from Exercise 1;
   note any shift.
2. **Outcome scoreboard.** Tight table: each KR from
   Exercise 3 with current value, target, midpoint
   confidence, two-sentence *what we've learned*.
3. **Bets in flight, and their falsifiers.** For each
   coherent action from Exercise 1 (and the platform bet
   from Exercise 4), the falsifier and its status.
4. **Deferrals that have re-surfaced.** From the Not-Now
   column of Exercise 2, any deferrals that have
   generated pressure; the CPO's answer and reasoning.
5. **Risks and what would invalidate the strategy.** Two
   or three plausible signals that would cause the CPO to
   escalate a strategy revision.
6. **Asks.** What the CPO needs from board / CEO — a
   hiring approval, a positioning decision, a customer
   introduction, a resolution to a cross-functional
   dispute.

**Must not contain.** Feature-list rundowns, sprint / ship
dates for outputs, roadmap-tool screenshots, team names
below the outcome layer unless one is the ask.

## Read-out two — for the chosen peer head (1–2 pages)

Written memo. Two parts: the shared narrative and the
function-specific supplement.

### Shared narrative (half a page)

- The outcomes moving next quarter (from Exercise 2).
- The bets underneath them (from Exercise 1).
- The deferrals (from Exercise 2's Not-Now column) — the
  section peers most need, so they stop pursuing what
  isn't being built.
- The escalation path if peer planning surfaces a conflict.

### Function-specific supplement (half a page)

Pick the section that matches your chosen peer. Detail
per [Lecture 5](../lectures/05-three-audience-roadmap-communication.md#contents-of-the-function-specific-supplements):

- **For GTM.** Which segments this quarter's outcomes are
  aimed at; which customer requests the strategy is
  explicitly not going to fund; the messaging the roadmap
  supports (and doesn't); which enterprise pilots are on
  the plan; the pipeline shape the strategy assumes.
- **For engineering.** Which architectural bets underpin
  the quarter's outcomes; which platform / API investments
  are in vs deferred; sequencing constraints the CPO is
  aware of; cross-team dependencies the CPO wants
  engineering leadership to sequence.
- **For design.** Which discovery / research work is in
  flight; the design bets that go with each outcome; the
  discovery evidence the CPO is currently trusting most
  and where it is weakest; surface-consistency questions
  raised by the platform-vs-point-product bet.

## Read-out three — for a product-team (one page + a short walkthrough plan)

Written team memo, plus a one-paragraph plan for the live
walkthrough where the CPO takes questions. Per
[Lecture 5](../lectures/05-three-audience-roadmap-communication.md#read-out-three--for-the-product-team-ics):

- **The outcome the team owns this quarter,** with the
  discovery evidence, the customer quotes, and the
  reasoning underneath. The *why* has to be in the memo,
  not just the *what*.
- **The falsifier the CPO is watching** for this outcome
  — so the team can raise their hand if they see it
  tripping from the inside.
- **The deferrals — and why.** So the team knows why they
  are not being asked to work on the thing a customer or
  stakeholder is pushing for.
- **The interfaces to other teams.** Which adjacent teams'
  work theirs depends on and vice versa.
- **What's changed since last read-out.**
- **What the CPO is unsure about.** The section that
  builds trust. Skip it and the memo reads as a
  memo-from-management.

**Voice check.** Read the memo out loud. If it sounds like
a board deck, rewrite it. The team can tell.

## The cross-artifact consistency check (half a page)

Before you consider the three read-outs done, run the check
from
[Lecture 5](../lectures/05-three-audience-roadmap-communication.md#the-cross-artifact-consistency-check).
Write half a page answering:

- **Outcome consistency.** Do the outcomes named in the
  three read-outs all trace back to the same source
  roadmap (Exercise 2) and OKRs (Exercise 3)? Note any
  drift and fix it in the source, not in one of the
  read-outs.
- **Deferral consistency.** Do the deferrals named in each
  read-out agree? A quiet mismatch is how stakeholders
  discover they can lobby for something the team was told
  was deferred.
- **Change-log consistency.** Have all three read-outs
  disclosed the same changes since the previous read-out?
  If the board is told the strategy is unchanged and the
  team is told they are re-orienting, someone is being
  misled.

If the check flags a discrepancy, note whether you fixed
it in the source (the strategy / roadmap / OKRs) or in the
read-out. Fixes in the source of truth are the right ones;
fixes in the read-out are patches that will drift again
next quarter.

## Starter guidance

- **Draft the team read-out first.** You are closest to the
  team; the empowering *why* is easier to write when
  you're not thinking about board polish. Then the peer
  read-out (planning-cycle-aware). Then the board
  read-out (governance-scoped). Draft order does not have
  to be publication order.
- **Optimize each read-out for its reader's decision.**
  Board = governance decision; peer = alignment decision;
  team = motivation and calibration decision.
  Re-read each and ask: *is this the shortest thing that
  answers that decision cleanly?*
- **Deferrals are load-bearing in all three read-outs.**
  The board sees deferrals so they see the CPO defending
  the discards. The peers see deferrals so they stop
  pursuing them. The team sees deferrals so they know why
  they're not on that work.
- **The team read-out includes the CPO's uncertainty.** The
  peer and board read-outs are more measured on this —
  peers need to plan against a defined direction, boards
  need governance clarity — but the team read-out gains
  trust when it names what the CPO is not sure of.

## Acceptance criteria

- Three separately-authored artifacts, each written for its
  reader's decision.
- Board read-out has all six sections above; no ship dates
  for outputs; contains at least one ask.
- Peer read-out is one shared narrative plus a
  function-specific supplement for the chosen peer; deferrals
  are named.
- Team read-out includes the *why*, the falsifier, the
  deferrals, the interfaces, the change-log, and the CPO's
  own uncertainty; voice is team-native, not board-polished.
- Cross-artifact consistency check answers all three
  questions; discrepancies are logged and — for anything
  fixable — noted as fixed in the source of truth.

## Common failure modes

- **Three read-outs, one voice.** Author writes the board
  read-out first, softens it for peers, softens it more for
  the team. Each is a board-deck voice adapted to a
  smaller stage. The corrective is to draft the team memo
  first.
- **Same document, three fonts.** Repackaging the strategy
  memo as three-different-looking PDFs is not authoring
  three read-outs. Different sections, different
  emphasis, different decisions.
- **The team read-out is a board deck.** If the team can
  pattern-match your team memo to something they'd have
  seen in a board deck, the discipline has failed.
- **The peer supplement is boilerplate.** If the GTM
  supplement is generic marketing-speak with no segment
  specifics, GTM will not be able to plan against it.
- **The consistency check is skipped.** Then it happens in
  a hallway conversation with a peer who noticed the
  discrepancy, which is the worst possible surfacing.
- **The board asks section is empty.** Board read-outs
  without asks train the board to stop reading; asks with
  context train them to keep reading.

## Source alignment

The three-audience discipline is standard executive-
communication practice, most rigorously argued in Barbara
Minto, *The Pyramid Principle* (Financial Times / Pearson,
3rd ed., 2010 —
[barbaraminto.com](https://www.barbaraminto.com/)). Camille
Fournier's *The Manager's Path* (O'Reilly, 2017 —
[oreilly.com](https://www.oreilly.com/library/view/the-managers-path/9781491973882/))
covers the peer-planning-alignment discipline; Marty Cagan's
*Empowered* (Wiley, 2020 —
[svpg.com/empowered](https://www.svpg.com/empowered-ordinary-people-extraordinary-products/))
covers the team-empowerment discipline; Amazon's public
six-pager tradition — see the 2016 shareholder letter
[aboutamazon.com](https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/company-news/2016-letter-to-shareholders)
— is the shape most founding CPOs should imitate for the
board / CEO read-out.
