# Exercise 2 — Outcomes-based roadmap authoring

**Time:** ~3 hours. **Deliverable:** one outcomes-based roadmap
in Now / Next / Later shape *or* objective-oriented shape, plus a
half-page memo explaining the shape choice and naming the
audit-test and instrumentation-test outcomes.

## Purpose

Turn the strategy from
[Exercise 1](exercise-01-rumelt-diagnosis-guiding-policy-coherent-action-drill.md)
into the physical roadmap artifact — a Now / Next / Later or
objective-oriented shape as covered in
[Lecture 2](../lectures/02-outcomes-based-roadmap-shape.md).
The practice is in translating the strategy's guiding policy
and coherent actions into outcomes that are shaped, instrumented,
and dated as outcomes (not shipments), and in making the
deferrals first-class rather than hidden.

## What you need before you start

- The strategy memo from
  [Exercise 1](exercise-01-rumelt-diagnosis-guiding-policy-coherent-action-drill.md).
  If you skipped Exercise 1, the roadmap will silently become a
  themes list; do Exercise 1 first.
- The ranked opportunity portfolio and the multi-surface
  allocation memo from
  [mod-002 Exercises 1 and 3](../../mod-002-opportunity-assessment-and-prioritization/exercises/exercise-01-opportunity-assessment-authoring-for-three-real-bets.md)
  if you have them; the roadmap is where those artifacts
  become a shared document.

## Choose the shape (five minutes)

Pick **Now / Next / Later** or **objective-oriented**. The
rule of thumb from
[Lecture 2](../lectures/02-outcomes-based-roadmap-shape.md#now--next--later--the-pre-scale-default):

- **Now / Next / Later** — pre-Series-A, one to four squads,
  discovery loop still visibly changing the picture.
- **Objective-oriented** — multiple squads, multiple concurrent
  outcomes to move, and enough delivery maturity that teams can
  actually own outcomes.

If you're on the boundary, pick Now/Next/Later and note in the
half-page memo why you chose it — the sharper artifact usually
teaches more.

## What the roadmap artifact must contain

### Now / Next / Later shape

Four columns (yes, four — the Not-Now column is required).
Three to five entries per column. Each entry is an outcome,
not a shipment.

- **Now (currently investing).** Outcomes teams are actively
  moving. Per entry: the outcome (with current value, target,
  horizon), the accountable team, the current bet on how it
  will move, and the falsifier that would end this line.
- **Next (probable, in believed order).** Outcomes the CPO
  believes will be picked up next as Now items finish or
  falsify. Per entry: the outcome (target, horizon), the
  reason it's Next rather than Now, and the discovery evidence
  that supports it.
- **Later (visible direction, not close).** Outcomes named as
  part of the strategy but not yet close to capacity. Per
  entry: the outcome (rough horizon — a half or a year),
  the reason it's Later rather than Next.
- **Not Now (explicitly deferred).** Items the strategy has
  chosen not to fund this year. Per entry: what is being
  deferred, the strategy-level reason (a sentence from the
  guiding policy), and the move-in signal that would move
  it back into consideration.

### Objective-oriented shape

A matrix where rows are outcomes and cells name the
accountable team and current bet.

- **Rows.** Three to six outcomes for the current horizon,
  each with a current value, a target value, and a dated
  outcome (per Lecture 2's discipline).
- **Cells.** For each outcome / horizon cell, the
  accountable team, the current bet in a phrase (not a
  feature list), the discovery evidence link, and the
  falsifier.
- **A Not-Investing row.** At least three deferred
  outcomes, each with reason + move-in signal.
- **Horizons.** This-half / next-half / exploratory is a
  good default. Do not use calendar months as columns —
  that recreates the calendar-shaped failure Lecture 2 is
  written against.

## The audit-test and instrumentation-test pass

Before you consider the roadmap done, run the two tests from
[Lecture 2](../lectures/02-outcomes-based-roadmap-shape.md#the-outcome-not-the-output--perris-discipline)
on every entry:

- **The audit test.** For each outcome: *if we shipped every
  feature we're currently imagining under this outcome and
  the outcome number did not move, would we still call this
  a success?* If yes, the entry is an output with an outcome
  label. Rewrite it, or split it into an output (delivery
  plan) and the underlying outcome you *actually* care about.
- **The instrumentation test.** For each outcome: *can we
  measure this outcome today?* If not, is the instrumentation
  itself on the roadmap? If neither, the outcome is
  decorative. Add the instrumentation as its own outcome, or
  admit the outcome is a wish and mark it as such.

Both tests are the discipline that separates the
outcomes-based roadmap from a themes list wearing an outcomes
label.

## The half-page memo

After the roadmap artifact, write a short memo. Answer:

- **Which shape did you pick and why?** Cite the rule of
  thumb from Lecture 2 and your specific product's stage.
- **Which outcomes failed the audit test on your first
  draft?** How did you rewrite them?
- **Which outcomes failed the instrumentation test?** Is the
  instrumentation on the roadmap? If not, why?
- **What deferrals were hardest to name in writing?** The
  deferrals that hurt to write are usually the ones the
  strategy most depends on — they are the visible discards
  from
  [Exercise 1](exercise-01-rumelt-diagnosis-guiding-policy-coherent-action-drill.md).

## Starter guidance

- **Write outcomes with a current value AND a target.** An
  outcome without a current value is impossible to grade at
  the end of the horizon; an outcome without a target is
  impossible to plan against.
- **One primary outcome per team per horizon.** If you have
  three outcomes assigned to one team in the same horizon,
  the team's own prioritization work is not yet done — force
  the primary and demote or move the others.
- **Do not put ship dates on outputs.** Dates go on the
  *outcome*, not on the feature. Ship-date planning is the
  delivery plan (mod-004), not the strategy roadmap.
- **The Not-Now column is not a graveyard.** Each Not-Now
  entry needs a move-in signal — otherwise it's a place to
  hide things you didn't want to argue about.
- **Steal the format from an existing tool if you like, but
  own the content.** Notion / ProdPad / Productboard / Aha
  templates are all fine. None of them save you from
  authoring the outcomes.

## Acceptance criteria

- The roadmap is Now/Next/Later or objective-oriented, with
  the choice explained in the half-page memo.
- Every outcome has a current value, a target value, a
  horizon, and a named instrument (or the instrumentation
  is itself an outcome on the roadmap).
- Every outcome passes the audit test — no output-shaped
  entries hiding under outcome labels.
- The Not-Now column / row has at least three deferrals,
  each with reason and move-in signal.
- The strategy's guiding-policy tie-breaker (from
  Exercise 1) is discernibly in operation across the
  ordering — a stranger reading both artifacts could tell
  the roadmap is generated by the strategy.
- Dates are on outcomes, not on outputs.

## Common failure modes

- **Now/Next/Later mapped to Q1/Q2/Q3.** Recreates the
  calendar-shaped failure. The three columns are about *how
  close to committed*, not about *which quarter*.
- **Outcomes without targets.** *"Improve activation"* is a
  theme; *"move week-1 activation from 22% to 45%"* is an
  outcome.
- **The Later column as a wish holder.** If items sit in
  Later for a year with no evolution, retire them or
  promote them.
- **Every team owns every outcome.** If team ownership is
  fuzzy the roadmap will resolve every dispute by
  meeting; make one team primary per outcome and note the
  supporting teams.
- **Deferrals hidden in prose.** Not-Now is a first-class
  column / row on the artifact, not a footnote.
- **Instrumentation debt hidden.** If you cannot measure an
  outcome and the instrumentation is not on the roadmap,
  the outcome is decorative — you have declared it because
  it sounds better than the feature list.
