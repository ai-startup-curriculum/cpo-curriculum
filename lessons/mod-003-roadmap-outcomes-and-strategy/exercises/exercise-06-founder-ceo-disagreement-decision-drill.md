# Exercise 6 — Founder-CEO disagreement decision drill

**Time:** ~2 hours. **Deliverable:** a one-page decision-rights
table for the CPO / founder-CEO relationship, plus a written
disagreement memo on one real (or plausibly-synthetic) roadmap
call where the CPO and founder-CEO see it differently.

## Purpose

Practice the two mechanics from
[Lecture 6](../lectures/06-founder-ceo-disagreement.md) that
most sharply distinguish founding CPOs who last from ones who
become Jira admins or quit within six months: **written
decision rights** authored before there's anything to disagree
about, and the **disagree-and-commit memo** as the artifact
that forces the disagreement into a discussable form before it
becomes a hallway argument.

## What you need before you start

- The strategy from
  [Exercise 1](exercise-01-rumelt-diagnosis-guiding-policy-coherent-action-drill.md),
  the roadmap from
  [Exercise 2](exercise-02-outcomes-based-roadmap-authoring.md),
  and the platform bet from
  [Exercise 4](exercise-04-platform-vs-point-product-bet-drill.md).
  The disagreement should be a real call that lives inside
  one of those documents.
- A real founder-CEO relationship, or a plausible-synthetic
  one you can defend. If synthetic, write half a page on the
  CEO — technical background, tenure, current biggest
  concern, communication style — before you author the
  disagreement.

## Part 1 — The decision-rights table (30 minutes)

Use the DACI shape from
[Lecture 6](../lectures/06-founder-ceo-disagreement.md#decision-rights--write-them-down-before-you-need-them)
— Driver, Approver, Consulted, Informed — for the
roadmap-relevant decision types. Author a one-page table
covering, at minimum:

- Company diagnosis (Rumelt Level-1) — usually
  founder-CEO Driver, board Approver.
- Product diagnosis (product-scope) — usually CPO Driver,
  founder-CEO Approver.
- Guiding policy (product-scope).
- Coherent-action list (in-quarter).
- Deferrals (Not-Now list).
- Platform-vs-point-product bet.
- Team OKRs (per team).
- CPO-scope OKRs (quarterly).
- Kill decisions (in-quarter).
- Strategy revision (mid-quarter).

For each row, name Driver, Approver (if any), Consulted, and
Informed. Justify any deviation from the reference table in
Lecture 6 in a one-line note next to the row.

**Reality-check the table.** After authoring, count how many
rows have the founder-CEO as Approver. If it is *every* row,
you have written a Jira-admin contract, not a CPO contract.
Revise until at least the coherent-action, deferrals, kill
decisions, and team OKR rows are CPO-Driver with no Approver
(or with peer-consulted only).

## Part 2 — The disagreement memo (90 minutes)

Pick one specific roadmap call from your Exercise 1 / 2 / 4
work where the CPO and founder-CEO actually see it
differently — or plausibly could. Good candidates:

- A guiding-policy call: which segment to concentrate on
  this year.
- A platform-vs-point-product bet call: the bet you made in
  Exercise 4, if the founder-CEO would prefer the other
  direction.
- A deferral call: the founder-CEO wants to fund something
  the strategy has explicitly deferred.
- An outcome-vs-output call: the founder-CEO wants a ship
  date commitment on the roadmap that the outcome
  discipline would keep off it.

Write the memo in the five-section shape from
[Lecture 6](../lectures/06-founder-ceo-disagreement.md#the-written-disagreement-memo--the-exercise-6-shape):

### Section 1 — The call being made (one paragraph)

- State the CEO's position, in one sentence.
- State your position, in one sentence.
- State which disagreement category it is — diagnosis
  (category 1), guiding-policy (category 2), or
  coherent-action (category 3).
- State who is Driver and who is Approver per the table
  above.

### Section 2 — Your diagnosis-and-evidence (two paragraphs)

- What you believe the decisive challenge / policy /
  action is, and why.
- The evidence: discovery findings, retention / activation
  data, customer quotes, loss-reason analysis, aggregation
  read, whatever grounds your position.
- The falsifier that would tell you *you* are wrong.

### Section 3 — The CEO's position, steelmanned (one paragraph)

- Write the best version of the CEO's position you can.
  Not a strawman.
- If you cannot write a good version, the disagreement is
  not yet ready to escalate. Go back to the CEO for more
  detail before writing the memo.
- Note the strongest evidence the CEO has cited (or the
  strongest evidence they *would* cite, if the CEO in
  question is synthetic).

### Section 4 — What would move the call (one paragraph)

- Signals that would cause you to commit to the CEO's
  call voluntarily.
- Signals that would cause the CEO to commit to yours.
- At least one from each side, concrete and dated. This
  is the pre-registration discipline that keeps
  disagree-and-commit intellectually honest.

### Section 5 — Your commit-plan, if the call goes against you (one paragraph)

- What you will do to execute the CEO's call visibly.
- What you will say to the team in the read-out
  (Exercise 5's team read-out is the artifact).
- How you will monitor the falsifier you registered in
  Section 2, and what would trigger raising the
  disagreement again.

## Starter guidance

- **Author the decision-rights table before the
  disagreement memo.** If you do them in the reverse
  order, the memo will silently be shaped by the absence
  of a rights framework and the disagreement will read as
  a power move.
- **The steelman section is the point.** First-time CPOs
  rush past it. If you find yourself unable to write a
  good version of the CEO's position, that's a signal
  either (a) you don't fully understand it — go ask, or
  (b) the disagreement is category-3 masquerading as
  category-1 — revisit.
- **Pick a real disagreement, or a disagreement your
  synthetic CEO plausibly would hold.** A memo written
  against a strawman CEO teaches nothing.
- **The commit-plan should be actual.** *"I would commit"*
  is easy to write; the discipline is naming what you
  would do differently on Monday if the call went against
  you today.
- **Do not resolve escalation-worthy disagreements in this
  exercise.** If the disagreement is one of the two
  escalation cases from Lecture 6 (third-party commitment
  conflict, or evidence-falsification ask), the memo is a
  practice on escalation shape, not on
  disagree-and-commit — note that at the top and adjust
  Section 5 accordingly.

## Acceptance criteria

- One-page decision-rights table in DACI shape covering at
  least the ten decision types listed above.
- The CPO is Driver (no Approver, or only peer-Consulted)
  on at least the coherent-action, deferrals, kill
  decisions, and team OKR rows.
- Disagreement memo has all five sections in the order
  above.
- The disagreement is categorized (1, 2, or 3) and
  Driver / Approver named from the table.
- Section 3's steelman is a genuine best-form of the
  CEO's position, not a strawman.
- Section 4 has at least one concrete, dated signal from
  each side.
- Section 5 names actual behavior — what you would do,
  what you would say to the team, what you would monitor.

## Common failure modes

- **Every-row-Approver decision-rights table.** If the
  founder-CEO is Approver on every row, the CPO has no
  Driver authority. Correct until the day-to-day calls
  are CPO-owned.
- **Missing steelman.** The most common failure. Without
  the steelman, the memo is a case for your position,
  which is a debate document, not a disagreement memo.
- **No falsifier.** *"I believe X"* without *"and if Y
  happened I would change my mind"* is a position, not a
  disagreement worth resolving.
- **Vague commit-plan.** *"I would commit"* is not a
  plan; *"I would rewrite the team read-out to lead
  with the CEO's outcome, monitor the pilot-3 metric
  through end of Q2, and re-open the memo if the metric
  fell below X"* is a plan.
- **Category-mismatched escalation.** A category-3 call
  (a specific bet the CPO is Driver on) does not
  belong in a written disagreement memo — it should be
  made and previewed in the one-on-one, not
  litigated. If you find yourself writing memos for
  category-3 calls, the decision-rights table hasn't
  been internalized yet.
- **Disagree-and-*don't*-commit.** Writing the memo,
  losing the call, then quietly re-litigating it a
  month later is the failure mode
  disagree-and-commit is defined against. Section 5's
  visibility discipline is what prevents it.
