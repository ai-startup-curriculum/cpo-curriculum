# Resources — mod-001 Customer Discovery → Product/Market Fit

Primary sources for the material in this module. Prefer the books over the
blog posts for depth; prefer the essays and HBR articles when you need a
single-sitting refresher. Every URL below is either the author's canonical
page or an archived copy where the original has moved or lapsed.

## Customer Development (Blank)

- Steve Blank, *The Four Steps to the Epiphany*, K&S Ranch, 2005 —
  [Stanford E145 PDF (course handout)](https://web.stanford.edu/group/e145/cgi-bin/winter/drupal/upload/handouts/Four_Steps.pdf).
  The founding text: startups *search* for a business model, they don't
  execute one. Chapter 1 sets up the four-step Customer Development process
  used throughout this module.
- Steve Blank & Bob Dorf, *The Startup Owner's Manual*, K&S Ranch, 2012 —
  [Strategyzer library entry](https://www.strategyzer.com/library/the-startup-owners-manual).
  The operational manual version. Chapter 1 is the Customer Development
  overview cited in Lecture 1; the Customer Validation chapter is the
  reference for Anti-pattern 3 in Lecture 4 ("demo ≠ sale").
- Steve Blank, [steveblank.com](https://steveblank.com/) — long-running blog;
  the "Customer Development" tag has practitioner updates to the model.

## Lean Startup and MVP as discovery instrument (Ries)

- Eric Ries, *The Lean Startup*, Crown Business, 2011 —
  [theleanstartup.com/book](http://theleanstartup.com/book). Chapter 5 ("Leap")
  is the source for the value hypothesis / growth hypothesis pair used in
  Lecture 1. Chapter 6 ("Test") frames the MVP as a discovery instrument, the
  countermeasure cited in Anti-pattern 8. Chapter 7 covers vanity vs.
  actionable metrics (Anti-pattern 4).

## Interview craft (Fitzpatrick)

- Rob Fitzpatrick, *The Mom Test*, self-published, 2013 —
  [momtestbook.com](https://www.momtestbook.com/). Short, practical, cheap.
  Chapter 1 introduces the three rules used to structure Lecture 2 and
  Exercise 2. Read it end-to-end before running your first interview.

## Jobs-to-be-Done

- Clayton Christensen, Taddy Hall, Karen Dillon & David Duncan, "Know Your
  Customers' Jobs to Be Done," *Harvard Business Review*, September 2016 —
  [hbr.org/2016/09/know-your-customers-jobs-to-be-done](https://hbr.org/2016/09/know-your-customers-jobs-to-be-done).
  The "milkshake" reframing and the "hire a product to make progress"
  quotation used in Lecture 2.
- Anthony Ulwick, "Turn Customer Input into Innovation," *Harvard Business
  Review*, January 2002 —
  [hbr.org/2002/01/turn-customer-input-into-innovation](https://hbr.org/2002/01/turn-customer-input-into-innovation).
  The Outcome-Driven Innovation strand of JTBD; useful contrast to the
  Christensen framing when you're synthesizing interview data.

## Continuous discovery and opportunity solution trees (Torres)

- Teresa Torres, *Continuous Discovery Habits*, Product Talk, 2021 —
  [producttalk.org/continuous-discovery-habits](https://www.producttalk.org/continuous-discovery-habits/).
  Chapter 1 defines the "one interview per team per week, sustained" cadence
  quoted in Lectures 1 and 2. Chapter 6 ("Mapping the Opportunity Space") is
  the reference for the opportunity solution tree used in the Lab.
- Teresa Torres, "Why I Use Opportunity Solution Trees," Product Talk, 2016 —
  [producttalk.org/2016/08/opportunity-solution-tree](https://www.producttalk.org/2016/08/opportunity-solution-tree/).
  The original blog post; a good ten-minute orientation before you read the
  book chapter.

## Product organizations and discovery-vs-delivery (Cagan)

- Marty Cagan, *Inspired: How to Create Tech Products Customers Love* (2nd
  ed.), Wiley, 2017 — [SVPG book page](https://www.svpg.com/inspired-how-to-create-products-customers-love/).
  Chapter 3, "Product Discovery," is the source for the "at least half of
  your ideas won't work" claim cited in Lecture 1 and the discovery-routing
  argument in Anti-pattern 7.
- Silicon Valley Product Group essays,
  [svpg.com/articles](https://www.svpg.com/articles/) — Cagan and colleagues
  writing on discovery, product management, and org design.

## Measuring product/market fit

- Sean Ellis, "The Startup Pyramid," startup-marketing.com, 2009 —
  [archived at web.archive.org](https://web.archive.org/web/20180302113143/http://www.startup-marketing.com/the-startup-pyramid/).
  The canonical wording of the 40% survey question and the "who to survey"
  guidance used throughout Lecture 3.
- Rahul Vohra, "How Superhuman Built an Engine to Find Product/Market Fit,"
  *First Round Review*, 2018 —
  [review.firstround.com](https://review.firstround.com/how-superhuman-built-an-engine-to-find-product-market-fit/).
  The operational playbook for iterating on the Sean Ellis score by segment;
  cited in Lecture 3 and Anti-pattern 1.
- Marc Andreessen, "The Only Thing That Matters," pmarchive, June 2007 —
  [archived at web.archive.org](https://web.archive.org/web/20140705013620/http://pmarchive.com/guide_to_startups_part4.html).
  Andreessen's definition of PMF and the "you can always feel it" line
  quoted in Lectures 3 and 4.

## Business model framing

- Alexander Osterwalder & Yves Pigneur, *Business Model Generation*, Wiley,
  2010 — [Business Model Canvas at strategyzer.com](https://www.strategyzer.com/library/the-business-model-canvas).
  The canvas gives you a compact set of dimensions (customer, value,
  channel, revenue) whose cells the assumption map in Exercise 1 covers.

## User-research technique (recommended, not required)

- Steve Portigal, *Interviewing Users: How to Uncover Compelling Insights*
  (2nd ed.), Rosenfeld Media, 2023 —
  [rosenfeldmedia.com/books/interviewing-users-2nd-edition](https://rosenfeldmedia.com/books/interviewing-users-second-edition/).
  Deeper craft manual than *The Mom Test*: recruiting, consent, note-taking,
  and saturation. Useful once you've run five interviews and want to level
  up the practice.

## Boundary: where this module hands off

This module owns the **discovery** half of 0 → 1 — hypotheses, interviews,
synthesis, PMF measurement. The **sell-side** half of the same phase —
early sales motion, pricing to first customers, channel bring-up, cold
outbound craft — is owned by the peer track at level 30:

- [startup-product-gtm-curriculum](https://github.com/ai-startup-curriculum/startup-product-gtm-curriculum)
  — the 0 → 1 GTM curriculum. If you finish the discovery loop with
  in-segment fit and need to figure out how to sell repeatably, that
  curriculum is the next stop. See
  [CURRICULUM.md](../../CURRICULUM.md) in this repo for the full ownership
  rule and the deferrals table.

## Adjacent reading (light touch)

The following aren't cited in the module but are one-step neighbors that
show up in most founding-CPO reading lists:

- Ash Maurya, *Running Lean* (3rd ed.), O'Reilly, 2022 —
  [ashmaurya.com/books](https://ashmaurya.com/books/). Lean-Canvas variant
  of the discovery loop; useful as a spaced-repetition read after Ries.
- Alberto Savoia, *The Right It*, HarperOne, 2019 —
  [rightit.com](https://www.rightit.com/). Pretotyping — cheap tests of the
  "would anyone want this?" question before you build.
- Y Combinator, [startupclass.samaltman.com](https://startupclass.samaltman.com/)
  — Sam Altman's "How to Start a Startup" lectures; Paul Graham on why
  founders should do sales; Kevin Hale on how to talk to users.

If a claim in the module cites a source not listed here, treat it as a
`needs-research` gap and open an issue.
