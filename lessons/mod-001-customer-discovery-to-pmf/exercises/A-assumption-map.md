# Exercise A — Assumption map for your riskiest bet

**Time:** ~30 min. **Deliverable:** one page, roughly the shape below.

## Purpose

Turn the vague statement of what your product does into a written map of the
specific things that must be true for it to work. You will use this map as
the input to the interview scripts in Exercise B and the sprint in the Lab.

## Instructions

1. **State the bet** in one sentence: *for [segment], we solve [problem] with
   [approach], and they will [action / pay].*
2. **List every claim** the sentence assumes, one per row. Aim for 10–20 rows
   the first pass. Anything you catch yourself saying "well obviously…" about
   is a claim; write it down.
3. **Categorize** each claim as:
   - **customer** (who has the problem)
   - **problem** (that they in fact have it, that it hurts enough to act)
   - **solution** (that yours addresses it, that they can adopt it)
   - **channel** (that you can reach them at a cost that works)
   - **willingness-to-pay** (that they'll pay the price you need)
4. **Score** each claim on two axes:
   - **Confidence** — how sure you are, 1–5 (5 = would bet the company).
   - **Impact if wrong** — 1–5 (5 = kills the business).
5. **Rank** by `impact × (6 − confidence)`. The top rows are your riskiest
   bets — the ones discovery should test first.

## Template

| # | Claim | Type | Conf (1–5) | Impact (1–5) | Rank |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Independent tradespeople reconcile receipts weekly | customer | 3 | 5 | 15 |
| 2 | They currently use paper + spreadsheet, not accounting SaaS | problem | 2 | 5 | 20 |
| 3 | They will scan receipts from the job site if it takes < 10s | solution | 2 | 5 | 20 |
| … | … | … | … | … | … |

## Success criteria

- The top three risks are testable in an interview or in a week-long
  experiment (not "will the enterprise buyer sign a $500k deal"; that
  requires a validation phase, not a discovery interview).
- At least one row per type. If you have zero *channel* or *willingness-to-pay*
  rows, you have blind spots, not clarity.
- A skeptic reading the page can see, immediately, what would falsify the
  top-ranked claims.

## Common failure modes

- **Only solution claims.** You've re-described what you want to build,
  not what has to be true about the world for it to matter.
- **All confidences at 4–5.** You're anchoring on your prior beliefs.
  Re-score with a colleague who is willing to push back.
- **"Users will love it" as a claim.** Too vague to test. Rewrite as a
  behavior in a context: *users in [segment] will complete [action] within
  [time] of first exposure.*

## Source alignment

The map is a lightweight version of the Business Model Canvas's
"assumption" reading, made operational by Alexander Osterwalder & Yves
Pigneur, *Business Model Generation* (Wiley, 2010 —
[strategyzer.com/library/the-business-model-canvas](https://www.strategyzer.com/library/the-business-model-canvas)),
and it aligns with Steve Blank's Customer Development framing of the founder's
job as "list every hypothesis; get out of the building; test them" ([Blank &
Dorf, *The Startup Owner's Manual*, 2012, Chapter
1](https://www.strategyzer.com/library/the-startup-owners-manual)).
