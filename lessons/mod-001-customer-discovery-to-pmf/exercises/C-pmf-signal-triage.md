# Exercise C — PMF-signal triage

**Time:** ~45 min. **Deliverable:** a one-page memo with an honest verdict.

## Purpose

Practice reading a mixed bag of "PMF signals" without lying to yourself.
Real fit conversations are messy — the survey looks great and retention
looks bad, or vice-versa — and the founding CPO has to give a verdict
anyway.

## Scenario

You are 14 months into a B2B SaaS aimed at mid-market operations teams
(200–2,000 employees). The last quarter looked like this:

- **Sean Ellis survey**, blended across all users:
  - Very disappointed: 34%
  - Somewhat disappointed: 41%
  - Not disappointed: 25%
- **Segmented**: in the target segment (mid-market ops teams that use
  Salesforce), *very disappointed* is 51%; outside the segment it is 12%.
- **Retention**: weekly active-user retention flattens at 22% for cohorts
  that came in via the Salesforce integration; retention on cohorts from
  the product-hunt launch decays to 3% by week 6.
- **Growth**: sign-ups are flat quarter-over-quarter; paid sign-ups from
  cold outbound converted at 4% (industry median for the price point is
  ~3%). <!-- needs-research: verify an official industry benchmark for
  mid-market SaaS cold-outbound conversion rate; cite (e.g., Pavilion or
  Bessemer State of the Cloud) or drop the "industry median" comparator. -->
- **Word of mouth**: three of the last ten paid deals cited "a peer at
  another company recommended it." Two of the ten cited "we found you via
  a Google search."
- **Board narrative**: the deck last month claimed "clear product/market
  fit," citing the launch-week Product Hunt #2 finish and the
  quarter-over-quarter revenue growth.
- **Sales lead** says: "We can sell this. We just need more leads."
- **Support lead** says: "Our top-5 customers file more tickets than the
  next 50 combined, and they always want the same three integrations."

## Tasks

1. **Rate** each signal as *green*, *amber*, or *red*, and give your
   reasoning in one sentence. Use the segmentation rules from Lecture 3:
   never accept a blended number, always separate cold from warm cohorts,
   and separate qualitative pull from launch spikes.
2. **Write the verdict**: do you have PMF? Options are:
   - *In-segment PMF* (fit inside a specific segment; expand there, don't
     scale broad).
   - *Approaching PMF* (signals encouraging but at least one instrument
     not yet green).
   - *No PMF yet* (signals do not support the claim; keep discovering,
     don't scale).
3. **Prescribe next quarter's work** — in three bullets, aligned to your
   verdict. If in-segment, what do you concentrate on? If approaching,
   what specifically closes the gap? If not yet, what discovery work
   fills the missing evidence?
4. **Draft the correction** to the board narrative. Two sentences, honest
   and defensible.

## Success criteria

- Your ratings distinguish the *segmented* Sean Ellis number from the
  *blended* one and act on the segmented view.
- Your ratings distinguish the *Salesforce-integration cohort* retention
  from the *launch cohort* retention and act on the difference.
- Your verdict names a segment, not the whole market.
- Your board correction does not use the phrase "clear product/market
  fit" unless you can defend it with two instruments over 8+ weeks.
