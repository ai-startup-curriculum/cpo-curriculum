# mod-001 — Customer Discovery → Product/Market Fit

> The founding CPO's first job isn't roadmapping. It's making sure the team is
> building something a real customer will pull out of your hands.

## Why this module exists

Startups die from building the wrong thing far more often than from building
the right thing badly. Steve Blank's founding argument for Customer Development
is that a startup is a *temporary organization designed to search for a
repeatable and scalable business model* — search, not execute — and the
CEO/founding CPO's job in that phase is to run that search with the same rigor
engineering brings to shipping ([Blank, *The Four Steps to the Epiphany*,
2005](https://web.stanford.edu/group/e145/cgi-bin/winter/drupal/upload/handouts/Four_Steps.pdf);
[Blank & Dorf, *The Startup Owner's Manual*,
2012](https://www.strategyzer.com/library/the-startup-owners-manual)).

This module gives you the vocabulary, the interview craft, and the measurement
regime that lets you tell — with evidence, not vibes — whether you have
product/market fit yet.

## Learning outcomes

By the end of this module you can:

1. **Frame** a customer-discovery program as a series of falsifiable
   assumptions rather than "let's go talk to users."
2. **Run** a discovery interview that yields evidence about past behavior, not
   speculation about future purchase intent — the specific failure mode Rob
   Fitzpatrick catalogs in *The Mom Test*
   ([Fitzpatrick, 2013](https://www.momtestbook.com/)).
3. **Synthesize** interview transcripts into a Jobs-to-be-Done narrative and an
   opportunity solution tree
   ([Christensen et al., "Know Your Customers' Jobs to Be Done," *HBR*, Sept
   2016](https://hbr.org/2016/09/know-your-customers-jobs-to-be-done);
   [Torres, *Continuous Discovery Habits*,
   2021](https://www.producttalk.org/continuous-discovery-habits/)).
4. **Measure** product/market fit with more than one instrument — the
   Sean-Ellis 40% survey, cohort retention curves, and organic pull — and
   articulate the limits of each ([Ellis, "The Startup Pyramid," 2009 —
   archived at
   startup-marketing.com](https://web.archive.org/web/20180302113143/http://www.startup-marketing.com/the-startup-pyramid/)).
5. **Diagnose** the classic anti-patterns: leading questions, false-positive
   demos, "vision" retention curves, launch metrics mistaken for fit.

## Prerequisites

Complete the customer/market segments in
[startup-foundations](https://github.com/ai-startup-curriculum/startup-foundations)
or bring equivalent experience:

- You can name one target segment in one sentence a stranger would understand.
- You have talked to (or can immediately arrange) at least five people in that
  segment.
- You have a running product prototype, a landing page, or a concierge/manual
  version of the service — *something* a person can react to.

## How to work this module

Read the lectures in order — they build vocabulary the exercises assume. Do the
exercises against a real product you're working on (or a proxy such as your
last employer's product) rather than an invented example; the failure modes
this module teaches are almost invisible in toy cases. Save the lab for a week
you can talk to five real humans; the point is the interviews, not the write-up.

## Deliverables

- Assumption map ([Exercise 1](exercises/exercise-01-assumption-map-for-riskiest-bet.md)) — a one-page grid of what must be true.
- Annotated interview script ([Exercise 2](exercises/exercise-02-interview-script-critique.md)) — your questions with red-lines.
- PMF-signal memo ([Exercise 3](exercises/exercise-03-pmf-signal-triage.md)) — one page, three signals, honest verdict.
- Discovery-sprint report ([Lab](labs/L-discovery-sprint.md)) — interview notes, an opportunity solution
  tree, and 2–3 next experiments with success/kill criteria.
